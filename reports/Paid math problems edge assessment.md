# Prize Math Pays Almost No Amateurs

**Bottom line: no.** A motivated individual with one or two RTX 4090s, heavy Claude/GPT-5-class usage and no research-math or security background has no realistic edge in paid math problems in late 2026, and the arenas that looked most promising two years ago have closed or hardened. XTX Markets shut the AIMO Prize in summer 2026 without ever awarding the $5M Grand Prize, having paid only **~$1.62M of $5.58M advertised** across three contests ([XTX Markets](https://www.xtxmarkets.com/news/2026-update-on-xtx-markets-ai-philanthropy/)); every documented Erdős, Millennium, Beal or Collatz payout in history went to professional mathematicians, and the entire unsolved Erdős pool is worth about **$38,670** ([erdosproblems.com](https://www.erdosproblems.com/prizes)); Kaggle's whole platform paid roughly **$3.7M across 68 competitions** in 2025, almost all of it to the top three to ten places ([ML Contests](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2025/)). The 2026 AI-for-math wave has changed who can *produce* a candidate proof (a 23-year-old with a ChatGPT Pro subscription got credit on an Erdős problem) but not who verifies, publishes or pays, and no cash reached that amateur. Consumer GPUs are neither an edge nor a blocker: organisers now cap inference on their own hardware and top teams train on 32 H100s, so local compute is irrelevant at both ends. Ranked by realistic expected money over the next twelve months, the only arena with a genuine income ladder is crypto/DeFi security (audit contests and Immunefi bounties), where the math edge maps onto rounding and precision bugs that have paid $100K–$1M, but even there the honest newcomer trajectory is a six-to-twelve-month unpaid ramp toward first payouts in the hundreds of dollars. The genuinely open, individual-eligible opportunities with money on the table right now are the Vesuvius Challenge's $20K monthly progress prize and ARC Prize 2026's Milestone #2 (both due 30 September 2026), the ongoing Sherlock/Cantina/CodeHawks contest calendar, and two days-old, under-collateralised Lean-formalization bounty schemes (Justin Sun Prize, conjectures.io) whose payment record is zero. Everything with six or seven figures attached is closed, institutional, or a lottery ticket gated by journal publication.

*Sourcing caveat: the researchers' egress proxy blocked direct reads of nearly every primary site (aimoprize.com, kaggle.com, erdosproblems.com, claymath.org, immunefi.com, arxiv.org, quantamagazine.org and others). Only GitHub pages were read in full; all other figures below come from search-engine excerpts of the cited pages and should be re-verified on the primary page before being quoted externally. Where two excerpts disagreed, both are noted.*

## The landscape shrank in 2026: AIMO, Code4rena and curl all closed

The single most important fact for this question is that the flagship "paid math problem" for AI builders no longer exists. XTX Markets closed the AIMO Prize, "including not progressing with the Grand Prize," after AIMO3 concluded in spring 2026; across the series **6,829 teams and 8,145 participants made 274,576 submissions** for approximately $1.62M actually awarded ([XTX Markets](https://www.xtxmarkets.com/news/2026-update-on-xtx-markets-ai-philanthropy/)). XTX's stated reasons are instructive: in AIMO3 "most teams used the same base model (gpt-oss-120b) and built upon the same notebook, which led to strong clustering of scores," the valuable outputs came from the organiser-led review process and side prizes rather than the leaderboard winners, and open-weight models now claim perfect IMO scores, so the frontier has "shifted definitively to research mathematics" ([XTX Markets](https://www.xtxmarkets.com/news/2026-update-on-xtx-markets-ai-philanthropy/)). In other words the sponsor concluded that the leaderboard format had stopped discriminating between competitors, which is precisely the condition under which a solo entrant's marginal effort is worth least. AIMO3's own arithmetic makes the base rate concrete: **4,133 teams competed for five leaderboard prizes** ($262,144 down to $16,384) plus $110,000 of side prizes, so roughly **0.12% of teams got main-prize money** and about 0.2% got anything ([aimoprize.com](https://aimoprize.com/updates/); [Kaggle AIMO3](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ai-mathematical-olympiad-progress-prize-3)). The paid list skipped ranks #4 and #6, which strongly suggests that finishing top-5 did not even guarantee payment once open-source and verification requirements were applied.

The same contraction hit the security side. Code4rena, the original crypto audit-contest platform with $20M+ lifetime payouts and 16,600 registered wardens, announced in May 2026 that it is winding down, with Immunefi absorbing its clients ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-05-13-immufefi-absorb-code4rena-bug-bounty-customers-shutdown-decision-401179); [m3dython](https://m3dython.com/blog/code4rena-review-2026)). curl ended its bug bounty on 1 February 2026 after its confirmed-vulnerability rate fell **below 5%** under a flood of AI-generated reports ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/curl-ending-bug-bounty-program-after-flood-of-ai-slop-reports/)); Bugcrowd saw submissions more than quadruple in a three-week window in March 2026, mostly false positives ([Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/security/bug-bounty-platforms-battle-ai-slop)); HackerOne paused its Internet Bug Bounty and GitHub moved to an invite-only VIP tier with a four-submission cap for newcomers ([Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/05/18/ai-slop-bug-bounty-programs-suspended/)). Immunefi now limits accounts with zero paid reports to **one submission per 24 hours** and bans AI-generated reports outright ([Immunefi support](https://immunefisupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/39568715723409-Updated-Submission-Rules); [Immunefi on X](https://x.com/immunefi/status/1600889765448273920)). The direction of every one of these changes is the same: the platforms are rationing access toward *known* researchers and away from unproven AI-assisted newcomers, which is the exact profile in question. On the grant side, the Humanity's Last Exam question bounty ($5,000/$500 per accepted question) closed in March 2025, the AI for Math Summer Fellowship's 2026 applications are closed, and the AI for Math Fund's 2026 main round closed on 10 April 2026 ([Wikipedia HLE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanity's_Last_Exam); [Renaissance Philanthropy](https://www.renaissancephilanthropy.org/ai-for-math-fund)).

## Pure-math prizes have never paid a non-specialist, and AI has not changed that

The historical ledger is stark. Every documented Erdős payout went to career mathematicians: $1,000 to Szemerédi in 1974, and the $10,000 prime-gaps prize to Maynard, Green, Ford, Konyagin and Tao; between 1998 and 2017 Graham and Chung paid out on **only three Erdős prizes** from the fund ([Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/cash-for-math-the-erdos-prizes-live-on-20170605/)). The only Millennium Prize ever awarded (Poincaré, 2010) was declined by Perelman ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Prize_Problems)); Beal's $1M has been unclaimed since it was raised to that level in 2013 ([AMS](https://www.ams.org/profession/prizes-awards/ams-supported/beal-prize-rules)); Bakuage's ¥120M Collatz prize has had no accepted claim since 2021 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bakuage-offers-prize-of-120-million-jpy-to-whoever-solves-collatz-conjecture-math-problem-unsolved-for-84-years-301326629.html)). The researchers found **zero documented cases** of anyone without a research-math background collecting any of these prizes, ever. Structurally this is not an accident: every one of them is gated by journal publication. The Combinatorics Foundation pays Erdős prizes "only after the publication of a solution in a reputable journal, with accompanying documentation verifying that Erdős offered that amount" ([Combinatorics Foundation](https://www.combinatoricsfoundation.org/erd%C5%91s-problems)); Clay requires publication in a refereed journal "of world-wide repute" plus **two years of general acceptance** before consideration ([Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/rules/)); Beal and Bakuage have equivalent clauses. A solo amateur with a candidate proof has no referee community, no co-author, and no route through that gate.

The 2026 AI wave is the strongest counter-argument and deserves a fair hearing. Since October 2025 AI has helped resolve roughly **100 Erdős problems**, and Liam Price, "a 23-year-old with no advanced mathematics training," entered problem #1196 as a single prompt to GPT-5.4 Pro on 13 April 2026 and got a proof no human had found in sixty years ([Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/why-the-legendary-erdos-problems-are-falling-to-ai-20260803/); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/amateur-armed-with-chatgpt-vibe-maths-a-60-year-old-problem/)). But look at what happened next: erdosproblems.com credits the result to "GPT-5.4 Pro (prompted by Liam Price)," Tao validated it, and the paper was written up with Tao, Lichtman and six others ([erdosproblems.com](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1196); [TheJustinSunPrize PR #1074](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/pull/1074)). No source records any cash to Price. The human contributed no mathematics, so this "edge" is available to every subscriber and confers nothing durable; and the problems that fell were disproportionately obscure ones that "may reflect obscurity rather than difficulty," per Tao's own wiki, with most AI resolutions being "sophisticated literature searches" rather than new ideas ([teorth wiki](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems); [Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/why-the-legendary-erdos-problems-are-falling-to-ai-20260803/)). Tao estimates only **1–2% of open Erdős problems** are simple enough for current AI to solve with minimal human help, and an independent database of attempts finds success rates of the same 1–2%, clustered on easier problems ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/terence-tao-says-gpt-5-2-pro-cracked-an-erdos-problem-but-warns-the-win-says-more-about-speed-than-difficulty/); [arXiv 2601.22401](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22401v2)). That tractable tail is exactly what OpenAI (with its non-public "Astra" model producing ten results at roughly $2,000 of tokens each), DeepMind, Harmonic and Tao's collaborators have been systematically sweeping since late 2025 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/)). A consumer-tier user is racing frontier labs for the same crumbs, and the crumbs are worth $25–$500 each. The site's forum rules also forbid posting proofs one does not understand: "If you do not understand the mathematics yourself, please do not post it here" ([erdosproblems.com forum](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/AI%20Contributions?order=oldest)).

The Millennium headline of September 2026 confirms rather than contradicts this. OpenAI announced a Lean-verified proof of finite-time blow-up for forced 3D Navier–Stokes (Clay options C/D) on 8 September, reportedly from ~10,000 agents over 88 hours; it stated it will not claim the $1M; Clay has not accepted the result; and the claim is in a priority dispute with NYU's Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic's Levent Alpöge ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02842-5); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/08/openai-math-solution-navier-stokes-credit)). The earliest possible award under Clay's rules is around 2028, to an organisation that has declined it. Nothing here is reachable by an individual.

## In AI competitions, the model you can run matters more than the GPUs you own

The consumer-GPU question has a clean empirical answer from AIMO3 and the ML Contests data: two RTX 4090s are table stakes, not an edge. Where organisers fix an inference budget, they supply the hardware. AIMO3 ran on one Kaggle-provided H100 for five hours over 50 problems, and one unaffiliated competitor iterated 56 notebook versions to a **41.5/50 private score entirely on Kaggle's free compute**, never mentioning a personal GPU ([Jarvis2001/AIMO3-Writeup](https://github.com/Jarvis2001/AIMO3-Writeup)). That score was medal range but not top-5. Where training compute is unconstrained, the podium is set by datacentre hardware: ARC Prize 2025's winner NVARC (two NVIDIA researchers) and the WSDM Cup 2025 winner each trained on **four 8×H100 nodes**, and H100 dethroned A100 as the most-used GPU among 2025 winning solutions ([ML Contests](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2025/); [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-kaggle-grandmasters-win-artificial-general-intelligence-competition/)). Consumer cards do appear (RTX 4090/3090 were the most common consumer cards among 2024 winners; the Vesuvius First Letters prize was won on a GTX 1070) but the documented winning pattern with them is to rent cloud GPUs at the end ([ML Contests 2024](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2024/); [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-deciphers-scroll/)). The gpt-oss-120b model that won AIMO3 needs far more VRAM than 48 GB in any practical quantization, so a 4090 rig is useful for sub-30B experiments and nothing more.

More damaging to the "heavy AI-tool user" thesis is what AIMO3 revealed about where the leverage sits. A systematic analysis of 23+ experiments found that **"model capability dominates"**: the 8.3-point gap between gpt-oss-120b and gpt-oss-20b was four times larger than any prompt-level optimization (±2 points), "every prompt-level intervention fails," and the remaining gap to pass@20 is selection loss that "prompt engineering cannot" close ([arXiv 2603.27844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27844)). The layer where a solo AI-fluent generalist adds value is worth about two points, inside the noise of private-leaderboard luck; one 44/50 public notebook was titled "Skills optional, Luck required" ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/code/nihilisticneuralnet/44-50-aimo3-skills-optional-luck-required)). Autonomous agents are now competitors too: Meta FAIR's AIRA agent took gold (8th of ~4,000) in a live Kaggle reasoning competition, and OpenAI's MLE-bench showed an o1 agent medalling in 16.9% of 75 past Kaggle competitions ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/aira3-gold-medal-nvidia-kaggle-competition/); [arXiv 2410.07095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07095)).

The Kaggle base rate for a strong-but-non-elite competitor is close to zero cash. A typical competition runs three months for a **$25K–$100K purse split among 3–10 places out of 1,000–4,000 teams**; top 10% earns a bronze medal and nothing else; Grandmaster status requires five golds and is held by roughly 400 of 6M+ members ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/kaggle-competitions-the-complete-guide); [Kaggle Progression](https://www.kaggle.com/progression/competitions); [NVIDIA KGMoN](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/kaggle-grandmasters/)). A Kaggle Grandmaster's own verdict is that prize money "is usually not worth the time and effort you have to put in to win" ([Forecastegy](https://forecastegy.com/posts/are-kaggle-competitions-worth-it-ponderings-of-a-kaggle-grandmaster/)). ARC Prize 2025 drew 1,455 teams and paid just over $125K in total, with the $700K grand prize unclaimed, and all three podium places went to NVIDIA, the returning 2024 winners and a corporate lab ([ARC Prize](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis)). The steelman is real but narrow: ML Contests data show roughly half of competition winners competed solo (2022) and more than half of winning teams are first-time winners (2023), so the market rewards skill rather than incumbency ([ML Contests 2022](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-competitive-machine-learning-2022/); [ML Contests 2023](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-competitive-machine-learning-2023/)); and ARC 2024's top five included three single-handle entrants and two PhD students at the top ([ARC Prize on X](https://x.com/arcprize/status/1865106288012623887)). The pattern in every outsider win (Vesuvius 2023, ARC 2024) is early entry into a *new* prize before professionals arrived, plus hundreds of hours. By the following year each podium had consolidated to institutions.

That leaves the side-prize and open-source tracks as the only rational targets. XTX itself said AIMO3's write-up, hard-problem and corpus prizes produced most of the value; ARC 2026 has a $375K paper-track pool split equally among all papers scoring above 4.5/5 and a $275K grand-prize write-up award ([Kaggle ARC paper track](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-paper-track); [ARC-AGI-2](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026/arc-agi-2)); the Vesuvius Challenge pays **$20,000 guaranteed every month** to the best open-source progress submission from a "team or individual" and has awarded $1.868M to date ([Vesuvius Open Prizes](https://scrollprize.org/prizes)). These are judged qualitatively, draw far smaller fields than leaderboards, and are where an individual with good tooling can actually compete. The realistic annual expectation, assuming one top-3 finish in one small event, is in the **$2.5K–$30K** range, with the median outcome being zero.

## Security bounties are the only ladder, and the rungs are far apart

Crypto and software bug bounties differ from every other arena in one respect: money flows continuously, to thousands of people, on a merit basis that does not require credentials. Immunefi has paid **~$140M lifetime** across 650+ protocols, HackerOne paid **$81M in the year to June 2025**, Cantina reports $50M, HackenProof $26M ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-07-09-crypto-hack-losses-fall-below-1-billion-in-h1-2026-even-as-attack-volume-hits-record-immunefi-407707); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackerone-paid-81-million-in-bug-bounties-over-the-past-year/); [Cantina](https://cantina.xyz/opportunities/ended); [HackenProof](https://hackenproof.com/blog/bug-bounty-investment-case)). That is also the arena where the "math edge" has a documented cash mapping: rounding, precision and fixed-point errors in DeFi accounting are the best-paying math-heavy bug class on record, with The Graph paying **$290,497** for two rounding errors (2024), Balancer $250K and $1M for rounding bugs, DFX Finance $100K for a decimal-scaling truncation, and a $1M cross-chain 256-to-128-bit truncation bug in 2023 ([Immunefi/Medium](https://medium.com/immunefi/the-graph-rounding-error-bugfix-review-c946ff470f65); [tpiliposian/Immunefi-bugfixes](https://github.com/tpiliposian/Immunefi-bugfixes); [sayan011 writeups list](https://github.com/sayan011/Immunefi-bug-bounty-writeups-list)). Sherlock's judging guidelines note that "rounding and precision bugs in share/asset math show up constantly" and are treated as serious ([Sherlock docs](https://docs.sherlock.xyz/audits/judging/guidelines)). ZK soundness bugs pay less than their severity implies (zkSync Era's circuit soundness bug that could mint 100K ETH earned $50K; RISC Zero paid $50K), and pure cryptanalysis is not a bounty market at all: Claude Mythos's July 2026 breaks of HAWK and reduced-round AES carried no bounty and no deployed impact ([ChainLight](https://medium.com/chainlight/uncovering-a-zk-evm-soundness-bug-in-zksync-era-f3bc1b2a66d8); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses)).

The distribution, however, is brutal. Immunefi's **median confirmed payout is about $2,000** against a mean of $52,800; critical findings account for 87.8% of all dollars; the top 10 whitehats hold over $42M, roughly a third of everything ever paid; and the average critical payout fell 45% in 2025 to ~$25.6K ([Mitchell Amador](https://mitchellamador.com/p/the-roi-of-a-bug-bounty); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/354815/immunefi-launches-all-stars-elite-whitehat-program-amid-record-bybit-hack-rising-onchain-losses); [Jack Sanford on X](https://x.com/jack__sanford/status/1998729408857735202)). With ~92,000 registered researchers and ~1,100 paid reports per quarter, the lifetime payout per registrant is on the order of $3,000 and most registrants are never paid. HackerOne's last income survey found **12% of hackers make $20K+ per year, 3% make $100K+**, and the top 100 earners take ~39% of all payouts ([HackerOne 2020](https://www.hackerone.com/press-release/hacking-career-soars-popularity-according-hackerones-2020-hacker-report); [HackerOne HPSR](https://www.hackerone.com/press-release/hackerone-report-finds-210-spike-ai-vulnerability-reports-amid-rise-ai-autonomy)). Audit contests pay only for valid High/Medium findings with steep duplicate decay (Cantina: 10 points for a unique high, 4.5 each for two finders, 2.7 for three) across fields of 100–600 entrants, and a 2026 practitioner analysis says roughly **three of four submitted findings earn nothing** ([Cantina docs](https://docs.cantina.xyz/cantina-docs/cantina-competitions/judging-process/scoring); [dev.to](https://dev.to/holistis/what-actually-gets-paid-in-smart-contract-audit-contests-583o)). Immunefi competitions now reserve 20% of every pot for a pre-selected "All Stars" elite ([Immunefi support](https://immunefisupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/35347143414417-Immunefi-All-Stars-Everything-You-Need-to-Know)). Even valid criticals get paid at a fraction of the advertised cap (Arbitrum paid $780K of a $2M headline) or not at all; a community "Wall of Shame" tracks ~$2.5M in unpaid bounties, and "No Fix, No Pay" lets projects decline payment for bugs they choose not to fix ([Bug Bounty Wall of Shame](https://bug-bounty-wall-of-shame.github.io/); [Immunefi support](https://immunefisupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/22617181023889-No-Fix-No-Pay)).

What it actually takes is documented and slow. Published roadmaps put the first contest at months 6–9 of dedicated study, "probably finishing mid-table or lower" ([DeepStrike](https://deepstrike.io/blog/smart-contract-auditor-roadmap-2025)); the only sourced newcomer payout is **$445.93 for a 2nd place** in a small CodeHawks contest ([Cyfrin](https://www.cyfrin.io/success-stories/how-i-became-a-smart-contract-auditor-a-codehawks-success-story)); the credible full-time mid-tier figure is milotruck's ~$172K across 17 contests in 2023, after finding 70 high/medium bugs in 34,000 lines of Solidity ([milotruck](https://milotruck.github.io/blog/A-year-of-Competitive-Audits/)). AI helps with reading code and drafting hypotheses, but the programs have responded by treating unproven AI-assisted submitters as spam until they have a paid report, and the AI systems that succeed at scale (XBOW's ~1,060 reports in 90 days, Anthropic's 2,300 disclosures with Trail of Bits triage) run with industrial human validation layers a solo operator cannot replicate ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-ai-xbow-tops-hackerone-us-leaderboad/); [Anthropic CVD](https://red.anthropic.com/2026/cvd/)). The one individual AI success story, Joshua Rogers fixing ~50 real curl bugs with an AI scanner, came from an established researcher using AI as a filter ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/curl-maintainer-stenberg-says-ai-help-fix-dozens-of-bugs/)). Legally, the path is narrow but clear: test only in-scope assets on local forks (mainnet testing is a permanent-ban offence on Immunefi), submit privately before any disclosure, expect KYC, and never treat post-exploit "10% whitehat" deals as an earning route ([Immunefi Rules](https://immunefi.com/rules/)).

## Ranking the arenas and naming what is actually open

The table below ranks arenas by realistic expected money over the next twelve months for this specific profile. "Expected" means probability-weighted, so a $1M prize with a one-in-a-million chance is $1. Estimates are inferences from the sourced base rates above, not published figures.

| Rank | Arena | Realistic 12-month expected money | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Crypto audit contests (Sherlock, Cantina, CodeHawks) and Immunefi bounties | Low hundreds to low thousands, after a 6–12 month unpaid ramp; long-tail chance of a $20K+ critical | Only arena with continuous merit-based payouts; math edge maps to rounding/precision bugs; but median $2K, 75% of findings unpaid, AI-slop gating |
| 2 | Open-source and side-prize tracks (Vesuvius monthly $20K; ARC 2026 milestones and paper track) | Hundreds to a few thousand; $2.5K–$30K if one top-3 finish lands | Judged qualitatively, small fields, individual-eligible; Vesuvius has paid $1.868M to date |
| 3 | Lean-formalization bounties (Justin Sun Prize 30% formalizer share; conjectures.io $750 defect bounty) | Speculative; only the $750 defect bounty has a documented payment record | Days-old, crypto-funded, under-collateralised; registry shows zero confirmed awards |
| 4 | Kaggle leaderboards (general ML) | Near zero unless already gold-level | ~0.1–0.5% chance of cashing per competition; top places go to teams on H100 clusters |
| 5 | AI for Math Fund grants ($100K–$1M; $3M micro-grant pool) | Near zero in 2026 | Open to individuals but 2026 main round closed 10 April; ~10% acceptance; all named grantees are academics |
| 6 | Erdős prizes | $0–$500 ceiling; effectively zero | Journal-publication gate; frontier labs sweeping the tractable 1–2%; no amateur ever paid |
| 7 | AIMO | $0 | Closed, no successor |
| 8 | Millennium, Beal, Collatz | $0 | Journal + multi-year acceptance gates; no non-specialist has ever been paid |

Against that ranking, the following is the current status of each named opportunity as of 21 September 2026.

**Genuinely open and individual-eligible.** The Vesuvius Challenge has **$2.14M open** including an $800K grand prize and $100K/$50K/$50K for 2nd–4th, plus the $20K guaranteed monthly progress prize for open-source work that "makes any of the Open Problems easier," with the next deadline **30 September 2026** ([Vesuvius Open Prizes](https://scrollprize.org/prizes)). ARC Prize 2026 offers $2M across three tracks: ARC-AGI-3 Milestone #2 on **30 September 2026** pays $25K/$10K/$2.5K; the ARC-AGI-2 track has a $275K grand-prize write-up award and a $150K accuracy bonus split among top-5 teams above 85%; the paper track has $375K divided equally among papers above 4.5/5, and all leading entries must be open-sourced ([ARC Prize 2026](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026); [ARC-AGI-3](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026/arc-agi-3)). Sherlock, Cantina and CodeHawks contests run continuously with pots of $50K–$300K (Sherlock) up to $2M+ (Cantina), and Immunefi's live programs include ZK caps of $50K (zkVerify) to $1M (Ethereum Foundation, Polygon zkEVM) ([SmartContractsHacking](https://smartcontractshacking.com/tools/web3-auditing-competitions-and-bug-bounties/cantina); [ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/bug-bounty/); [Polygon](https://polygon.technology/blog/bug-bounty-for-polygon-zkevm)). The Erdős pool remains standing at ~$38,670 unsolved, most items $25–$1,000, payable only after journal publication ([erdosproblems.com](https://www.erdosproblems.com/prizes)). Epoch AI's FrontierMath Open Problems page accepts submissions, but explicitly from professional mathematicians' own research, and its earlier Tier 1–4 contracting paid $300–$1,000 per accepted problem ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems/submissions); [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/cu2E8wgmbdZbqeWqb/meemi-s-shortform)).

**Open but unproven (treat as speculative).** The Justin Sun Prize, announced 16 September 2026, offers up to **$1M per problem** across a 1,022-problem Lean-verified registry, splitting 70% to the prover and 30% to the formalizer, with claims filed by GitHub issue ([The Block, sponsored](https://www.theblock.co/news/sponsored/2026-09-16-justin-sun-establishes-the-justin-sun-prize-my-wealth-came-from-mathematics-and-will-return-to-mathematics-415271); [TheJustinSunPrize/awards](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards)). Press releases claim a $1M inaugural award to OpenAI, but the registry's own confirmed-awards page, read directly, states **"No awards are recorded yet"**; per-problem amounts are unpublished; press coverage and the award-process document contradict each other on whether pre-registry solutions qualify; and early amateur self-nominations for formalization shares, including one explicitly built "with AI assistance (Anthropic Claude)," were closed "not planned" ([awards/README.md](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/main/awards/README.md); [issue #381](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/issues/381)). The Bittensor Subnet 66 "conjectures.io" scheme quotes **$3,341–$3,810 per Erdős target** for a machine-checked Lean proof, but 253 live quotes summing to ~$937,669 sit against a treasury of ~$50,664 (about 5% collateralisation), payouts are in a volatile alpha token, each attempt costs a 0.25–0.5 TAO submission fee regardless of outcome, and several submissions were rejected because the problem was already solved elsewhere; the only bounty with a documented payment record is a flat **$750 for demonstrating a formalization defect**, paid at least six times ([leaftensor/conjectures](https://github.com/leaftensor/conjectures); [conjectures-validator](https://github.com/conjectures-io/conjectures-validator)). That $750 defect bounty, which rewards finding mistakes in Lean statements rather than proving theorems, is arguably the single most accessible math-flavoured cash item found in this research, and it is tiny.

**Closed.** AIMO (all three progress prizes and the Grand Prize); Code4rena (May 2026); curl's bounty (February 2026); the HLE question bounty (March 2025); the NVIDIA Nemotron Reasoning Challenge (winners announced April 2026); the Pokémon TCG challenge (final stage September 2026); the AI for Math Fund 2026 main round (10 April 2026) and Summer Fellowship 2026 ([Renaissance Philanthropy](https://www.renaissancephilanthropy.org/careers/ai-for-math-summer-fellowship)).

**Institutional-only.** DARPA expMath (performer contracts for industry/academia), Google DeepMind's AI for Math Initiative (five named institutes), Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative ($10M from Alex Gerko funding staff positions), NSF MFAI, and XTX's broader $50M+ AI-for-maths commitment, all of which flow to institutions or named performers ([DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/expmath-exponential-mathematics); [Google](https://blog.google/technology/google-deepmind/ai-for-math/); [Lean FRO](https://lean-lang.org/fro/about/)). The AI for Math Fund is formally "open to individuals and teams worldwide," but its 2025 round accepted 29 of 280 applications, every publicly named grantee is university-based, and the $3M micro-grant pool's terms were never published in accessible sources ([Renaissance Philanthropy](https://www.renaissancephilanthropy.org/ai-for-math-fund)).

**Lottery tickets.** The six Clay problems ($1M each), Beal ($1M), Bakuage Collatz (¥120M), the $5,000 and $10,000 Erdős items, and ARC's grand prizes (unclaimed in 2025 at the 85% threshold). Each is gated by referee acceptance in top journals, which for problems of this notoriety means surviving editors inundated with crank submissions; AI-generated Collatz "proofs" were already circulating on Medium in 2026 and being ignored ([Medium/Ueoka](https://medium.com/@u.yoshiki.phys/breaking-collatz-conjecture-proof-finalized-b722d4af343d)).

## Conclusion

The question assumes that AI fluency plus consumer compute is a scarce combination. In late 2026 it is the default: 82% of bug-bounty hunters and 70% of HackerOne researchers already use AI, AIMO3 entrants converged on one model and one notebook, and every ChatGPT Pro subscriber can prompt an Erdős problem. Once an edge is universal it is not an edge, and the platforms and sponsors have responded exactly as one would predict, by closing formats that no longer discriminate (AIMO), rationing access to proven researchers (Immunefi, GitHub, curl) and moving money toward institutions and formal-verification pipelines. What still pays a solo individual is the same thing that paid Luke Farritor on a GTX 1070 and the ARChitects in 2024: hundreds of hours of specific, verifiable work in an arena that is new enough that professionals have not yet arrived. In September 2026 that describes exactly two things on the list, the open-source side prizes (Vesuvius, ARC milestones and papers) and, with heavy caveats about payment credibility, the brand-new Lean-formalization bounties. Neither is a living.

If the goal is money rather than recognition, the honest recommendation is to treat the math-prize arenas as zero and the security arena as a twelve-month apprenticeship with a lottery attached: pick one bug class (fixed-point and rounding errors in DeFi accounting, where the math background actually maps to $100K–$1M paid examples), work up through CodeHawks First Flights to Sherlock and Cantina contests on local forks, and aim for the single event that changes everything on these platforms, the first paid report. Everything else in this landscape, from the $1M Navier–Stokes headline to the $3,800 conjectures.io quotes, is either already spoken for or not yet backed by money that has actually reached anyone.
