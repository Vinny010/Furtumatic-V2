## Key Question 1: Kaggle in 2026 — prize pools, distribution, field sizes, solo vs team, and whether consumer GPUs are competitive

### Takeaway
Kaggle's whole platform paid out roughly $3.7M across 68 competitions in 2025 (half of them small "Community" competitions capped at $10K), so a typical money competition is a $25K–$120K pool split among only the top 3–10 places out of 1,000–4,000+ teams; the money-relevant 2026 events for a math/reasoning person are AIMO3 ($2.2M, now the last AIMO), ARC Prize 2026 ($2M across three tracks), NVIDIA's Nemotron Reasoning Challenge ($100K+), and the Pokémon TCG challenge ($240K+). Inference-only competitions (AIMO3, Nemotron, ARC) are run inside Kaggle's own hardware budget (e.g. one H100 for 5 hours), which levels the field against big-GPU teams, but the 2025 winners' survey still shows A100s as by far the most common training hardware among #1 finishers.

### Inferences
- With ~$3.7M spread over 68 competitions and half of those capped at $10K, the median Kaggle competition pays well under $60K, split among 3–10 places out of typically 1,000–4,000 teams; an individual's probability of cashing in any given money competition is on the order of 0.1–0.5% unless they are already at gold-medal level.
- AIMO3's prize geometry (1st $262K, 5th $16K, five leaderboard prizes plus $110K of side prizes) means roughly 10–15 payouts total from a $2.2M pool; the side prizes (write-up, hard-problem, corpus) were the most plausible individual targets, and XTX itself said those side prizes produced most of the value. That route is now closed with AIMO.
- Inference-only formats (AIMO's 1×H100/5h, Nemotron's LoRA-only submission, ARC's Kaggle runtime) neutralise the training-compute advantage; the remaining edge is prompt/agent engineering, verification tooling and model selection, which is exactly what a strong AI-tool user can do on consumer hardware. XTX's complaint that everyone converged on the same model and notebook is the flip side: the field is crowded and scores cluster, so tie-breaking luck matters.
- For classic tabular/vision/NLP Kaggle competitions, a single RTX 4090/5090 is enough to reach medals, but the 2024 survey shows winners disproportionately on A100s; renting a few hundred dollars of cloud GPUs at the end is a documented winning pattern.

## Key Question 2: Realistic expected earnings for a strong but non-elite (top-10%) Kaggle competitor

### Takeaway
There is essentially no cash below the top 3–10 places; a top-10% finish earns a bronze medal and nothing else. Outside leaderboard prizes, the only money reachable by non-podium individuals is special-category prizes (write-up, open-source/"progress" prizes, efficiency prizes) that some hosts add, typically $2.5K–$25K each.

### Cited Findings
- Cash prizes are host-defined and in the documented examples stop at 3rd place, or at 10th place in the largest Vesuvius example ($5K for 5th–10th) — [Kaggle Competitions Setup docs](https://www.kaggle.com/docs/competitions-setup); [Scroll Prize Substack](https://scrollprize.substack.com/p/increasing-kaggle-ink-detection-prizes)
- Top ~10% earns a bronze medal; medals carry progression points and reputational value only — [Kaggle Progression System](https://www.kaggle.com/progression/competitions)
- ARC Prize 2025: 1,455 teams submitted 15,154 entries; guaranteed prizes were $125K (Top Score $75K, Paper $50K), the $700K Grand Prize (85% threshold) went unclaimed; "over $125K in prizes were awarded to open-source solutions"; 90 papers were submitted and the paper prizes were expanded with 5 extra runners-up and 8 honourable mentions — [ARC Prize 2025 Results and Analysis](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis); [ARC Prize 2025 competition details](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2025)
- ARC Prize 2025 1st: NVARC (24.03%); 2nd: the ARChitects (16.53%), who improved on their 2024 winning system with a 2D-aware masked-diffusion model — [ARC Prize 2025 Results](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis); [Trelis Research on NVARC](https://trelis.substack.com/p/nvarc-2025-arc-prize-winners)
- Grandmaster opinion: prize money is "usually not worth the time and effort," and the real return is career value — [Forecastegy](https://forecastegy.com/posts/are-kaggle-competitions-worth-it-ponderings-of-a-kaggle-grandmaster/)
- The Vesuvius Challenge (outside Kaggle) is the notable exception that pays below the podium: monthly Progress Prizes with $20,000 guaranteed for the best submission and eligibility for "any contribution that makes any of the Open Problems easier"; $2.14M currently open including an $800K grand prize and $100K/$50K/$50K for 2nd–4th; over $1.868M awarded to date; next progress-prize deadline 30 Sept 2026 — [Vesuvius Challenge Open Prizes](https://scrollprize.org/prizes)

### Inferences
- Expected cash for a competitor who reliably finishes top 10% but rarely top 5 is close to $0 per competition on Kaggle; over a year of 4–6 serious entries the expectation is perhaps a few hundred to low thousands of dollars, dominated by the small chance of one podium or one special-category prize.
- The best expected-value targets for a non-elite solo are (a) special prizes judged on write-ups/open-source contributions (ARC paper track, AIMO's former side prizes, Nemotron "progress"/open-contribution awards, Vesuvius monthly prizes), where judging is qualitative and fields are far smaller than the leaderboard; and (b) hardware prizes (DGX Spark units) which are capped one per participant.

- DARPA, DeepMind, OpenAI and Lean FRO money is not reachable by direct application from an individual; the route is employment or subcontracting with a performer.

## Key Question 4: Paid bounties for Lean/mathlib formalization or AI-math dataset contributions

### Takeaway
There is no official mathlib bounty board; paid work is (a) per-problem contracting for benchmark datasets (Epoch's FrontierMath paid $300–$1,000 per problem; HLE paid $5,000/$500 for top questions but closed in 2025), and (b) two brand-new (Aug–Sept 2026), lightly-vetted bounty systems for machine-checked proofs of Erdős-type conjectures — the crypto-funded Bittensor "conjectures.io" subnet (headline ~$3.3K–$3.8K per target, $750 flat for formalization defects, but backed by only ~$50K treasury) and the Justin Sun Prize (up to $1M per problem, 70/30 solver/verifier split, self-nomination via GitHub).

### Cited Findings
- Epoch AI FrontierMath (Tiers 1–4): ~1,000 problems; pay per problem $300–$1,000 depending on quality; full-time contributors also got a base $2,000/month for an expected 2–4 months; 60+ mathematicians contributed; OpenAI funded it and holds access, which contributors were not initially told — [LessWrong, meemi's Shortform](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/cu2E8wgmbdZbqeWqb/meemi-s-shortform); [LessWrong, lessons from the OpenAI–FrontierMath debacle](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/8ZgLYwBmB3vLavjKE/some-lessons-from-the-openai-frontiermath-debacle); [Epoch AI clarification](https://epoch.ai/latest/openai-and-frontiermath)
- FrontierMath: Open Problems — problems are contributed by professional mathematicians from their own research; "the main cost ... is compensation to mathematicians"; there is a public "Submit a Problem" page; supported by Schmidt Sciences; expanded to 50 problems on 31 July 2026; only 3 solved by AI so far; the order-668 Hadamard matrix problem was marked solved on 12 Aug 2026 by an Anthropic researcher crediting three humans plus Claude — [Epoch AI Open Problems about page](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems/about); [Submit a Problem](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems/submissions); [Crypto Briefing on the 50-problem expansion](https://cryptobriefing.com/frontiermath-open-problems-benchmark-50-challenges/)
- FrontierMath Erdős: 68 open Erdős problems curated by Thomas Bloom and formalized in Lean, AI must prove/disprove within a fixed budget — [Epoch AI, Announcing FrontierMath Erdős](https://epoch.ai/latest/announcing-frontiermath-erdos)
- Humanity's Last Exam paid question authors from a $500,000 pool: $5,000 for each of the top 50 questions and $500 for the next 500; the follow-up bug-bounty ended 21 March 2025 — [Wikipedia, Humanity's Last Exam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanity's_Last_Exam); [Scale AI blog](https://scale.com/blog/humanitys-last-exam-results)
- Historic precedent: OpenAI paid undergraduates to formalize olympiad problems in Lean (mathzoo/miniF2F era) — [Lean Zulip archive, autoformalization thread](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/219941-Machine-Learning-for-Theorem-Proving/topic/autoformalization.3F.html)
- Google DeepMind's formal-conjectures repository (established Aug 2025) catalogues Lean 4 statements of open conjectures and is the source pool for the bounty systems below; contributing to it is unpaid — [Formal Conjectures paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13171); [GitHub mirror](https://github.com/BoltonBailey/formal-conjectures)
- Bittensor Subnet 66 "conjectures.io" (opened early Aug 2026): 260 targets (236 Erdős problems + 24 Ben Green open problems) copied verbatim from DeepMind's formal-conjectures; pays a bounty for a machine-checked Lean proof or counterexample; quoted bounties are $3,341.58 (56 targets) and $3,809.84 (197 targets), but "the headline bounty is roughly 18× the pool that backs it": 253 live quotes sum to ~$937,669 against a treasury of ~$50,664; payouts are in SN66 alpha tokens (~$0.60/α), locked at intake; submission fee 0.25 TAO (guide) or 0.5 TAO (validator description) regardless of outcome; a separate flat $750 bounty for demonstrating a formalization defect has been paid at least six times; several submissions were rejected because the problem had already been solved elsewhere — [GitHub leaftensor/conjectures guide](https://github.com/leaftensor/conjectures); [conjectures.io](https://conjectures.io/); [GitHub conjectures-validator](https://github.com/conjectures-io/conjectures-validator)
- Justin Sun Prize (announced 16 Sept 2026 by TRON founder Justin Sun; some outlets render it "Sun Yat-sen Award"): up to $1M per problem on a list of 66 problems (Sendov conjecture, Erdős distinct-subset-sums, many Erdős problems, plus formal verification of Poincaré, Riemann, Goldbach); open to anyone including AI systems; first to produce a solution that passes formal (Lean) verification wins; solver gets 70% and the formal verifier 30% (100% if one party does both); covers advances on/after 1 Jan 2026, and if a problem was solved earlier the verifier is still eligible; inaugural $1M went to OpenAI for a Navier–Stokes existence/smoothness proof formalized by "GPT-6 Astra" — [The Block (sponsored)](https://www.theblock.co/news/sponsored/2026-09-16-justin-sun-establishes-the-justin-sun-prize-my-wealth-came-from-mathematics-and-will-return-to-mathematics-415271); [Cointelegraph (press release)](https://cointelegraph.com/press-releases/justin-sun-establishes-the-justin-sun-prize-my-wealth-came-from-mathematics-and-will-return-to-mathematics); [KuCoin news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/sun-yat-sen-establishes-sun-yat-sen-award-with-1m-prizes-for-math-breakthroughs); [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/55d72756-f431-47f6-bb03-79090035ec2f)

- OpenAI's model disproved Erdős's unit-distance conjecture on 20 May 2026 (a $500 Erdős bounty problem); humans improved it within weeks — [Quanta, Why the Legendary Erdős Problems Are Falling to AI (3 Aug 2026)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/why-the-legendary-erdos-problems-are-falling-to-ai-20260803/); [MAA](https://maa.org/math-values/ai-solves-an-80-year-old-erdos-problem/)

- No official mathlib or Lean FRO bounty program was found; Mathlib's paid work is through the $5M Mathlib Initiative staff funding — [Lean FRO about](https://lean-lang.org/fro/about/); [Mathlib on lean-lang.org](https://lean-lang.org/use-cases/mathlib/)

### Inferences
- The steady, low-risk formalization money is contracting for benchmark builders (Epoch, Scale-style datasets) at hundreds of dollars per accepted problem; that requires research-level math credibility, and FrontierMath's Open Problems track explicitly wants professional mathematicians' own research problems.
- The new bounty systems are where "consumer GPU + strong AI tooling" maps most directly to cash, because payment is for a Lean-checked artefact regardless of who or what produced it. But both are days-to-weeks old, crypto-funded, and under-collateralised (conjectures.io's treasury covers ~5% of quoted bounties; the Justin Sun Prize's early formalization claims were being closed "not planned"), so expected value is speculative and there is a submission fee (0.25–0.5 TAO) per attempt on Subnet 66.
- The 30% "verifier" share of the Justin Sun Prize for formalizing an already-solved problem is the only place where a pure Lean formalizer (as opposed to a problem solver) is offered large money; the credibility of payment is unproven.

## Key Question 5: Other competition venues where compute + AI skill converts to prize money

### Takeaway
ARC Prize 2026 ($2M across ARC-AGI-2, ARC-AGI-3 and a paper track, with small milestone prizes of $25K/$10K/$2.5K on 30 June and 30 Sept 2026) and the Vesuvius Challenge ($2.14M open, $20K guaranteed every month) are the two open venues that pay individuals repeatedly below the grand-prize level; both require open-sourcing.

### Cited Findings
- ARC Prize 2026 offers $2M across three tracks; all leading participants must open-source to be eligible — [ARC Prize 2026](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026)
- ARC-AGI-3 track: Milestone #1 (30 June 2026) and Milestone #2 (30 Sept 2026) each pay 1st $25K, 2nd $10K, 3rd $2.5K — [ARC-AGI-3 competition](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026/arc-agi-3); [Milestone #1 blog](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2026-milestone-1); [Kaggle ARC-AGI-3](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3)
- ARC-AGI-2 track: $275,000 Grand Prize Write-up to the team with the highest-scoring solution write-up, plus a $150,000 accuracy bonus split among the top 5 teams that cross 85% on the private set — [ARC-AGI-2 competition](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026/arc-agi-2); [Kaggle ARC-AGI-2](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-2)
- Paper track: $375,000 bonus for papers scoring above 4.5/5 on the rubric, divided equally among all qualifying papers — [Kaggle ARC Prize 2026 Paper Track](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-paper-track); [ARC Prize 2026 Paper Prize](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026/paper)
- ARC Prize 2025 attracted 1,455 teams / 15,154 entries and 90 papers, and paid out just over $125K in total; 2024's winner (the ARChitects) used test-time training, and 2025's winner (NVARC) built on it with heavy synthetic data — [ARC Prize 2025 Results](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis); [ARC Prize 2025 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10904v1)
- Vesuvius Challenge: $2,140,000 open (grand $800K; $100K/$50K/$50K for 2nd–4th), monthly $20K guaranteed progress prizes for open-source work, "team or individual" eligible, $1.868M+ awarded to date, next deadline 30 Sept 2026 — [Vesuvius Open Prizes](https://scrollprize.org/prizes); [Vesuvius FAQ](https://scrollprize.org/faq)
- NVIDIA Nemotron Reasoning Challenge ($100K+ plus DGX Spark units) and the Pokémon TCG challenge ($240K + $80K final) are the other 2026 Kaggle money events relevant to reasoning/agents — see Question 1 citations.
- HLE-style question bounties ($5,000/$500 per accepted question) demonstrate that benchmark builders will pay individuals for hard, verifiable problems, but that program closed in 2025 — [Wikipedia HLE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanity's_Last_Exam)

### Inferences
- ARC's milestone prizes ($37.5K per milestone, three places) and Vesuvius's $20K/month are the most "repeatable" small pots; both are judged partly on open-source write-ups where an individual with good tooling can compete, but ARC's leaderboard has been dominated by teams with prior-year systems (ARChitects, NVARC).
- Across all venues found, total cash realistically open to an unaffiliated individual in a year — assuming a top-3 finish in one small event — is in the $2.5K–$30K range; the six-figure prizes (AIMO 1st, ARC write-up, Justin Sun) have so far gone to corporate or multi-person teams.
