## Key Question 1: Immunefi and similar platforms — total payouts 2024–2026, largest payouts, distribution, and how many researchers earn >$10k/year

### Takeaway
Immunefi has paid roughly $140M lifetime (crossed in June 2026), but annual flow is lumpy and heavily concentrated: 2025 was a weak year (Q4 2025 only $2.40M; average critical payout fell 45% to ~$25.6k) before a rebound in H1 2026 (~$13.45M across 837 valid bugs). The median paid report is ~$2,000; critical findings account for ~88% of all dollars; the top 10 whitehats hold >$42M of lifetime payouts (roughly a third of everything ever paid). No platform publishes how many researchers clear $10k/year; the available concentration data implies it is a small minority of the ~92,000 registered accounts.

## Key Question 2: Audit contests (Code4rena, Sherlock, Cantina, CodeHawks) — mechanics, pot sizes, prize splits, and realistic newcomer earnings

### Takeaway
Contests pay only for valid High/Medium findings, split a fixed pot by severity-weighted points with steep duplicate decay (Cantina: 10 → 4.5 → 2.7 points for 1/2/3 finders), and typically draw 100–600 participants for pots of $50k–$300k (Sherlock/Code4rena) or $200k–$2.5M (Cantina). Credible individual write-ups show newcomers earning hundreds of dollars in early contests and top-decile full-timers ~$170k/year; a 2026 practitioner analysis says roughly three of four submitted findings earn nothing. Code4rena itself shut down in May 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Mechanics and prize splits**
- Cantina scoring: "if there were duplicated findings for a high severity bug, instead of getting 10 points each, each would be awarded 4.5 points. In case of 3 duplicates, each person gets 2.7 points"; "the first valid submission will be rewarded an additional 20% reward"; example with 30% incentive: earliest valid gets $2,453.83, other duplicates $1,886.79 each; "Only Highs and Mediums earn from the Primary Prize Pool; a separate reserved pool covers Low/Info" — [Cantina docs: Scoring](https://docs.cantina.xyz/cantina-docs/cantina-competitions/judging-process/scoring); [Cantina docs: Judging Processes](https://docs.cantina.xyz/standards/judging)
- Sherlock: "only Medium and High vulnerabilities count for Issue Points, and there is no reward for submitting Low or Informational issues"; pot for an issue "getting shared among everyone who found it" — [Sherlock docs: How to Score Issue Points](https://docs.sherlock.xyz/audits/watsons/how-to-score-issue-points-in-a-contest); [Meeting the Payout Criteria](https://docs.sherlock.xyz/audits/watsons/meeting-the-payout-criteria)
- Sherlock duplicate rules: issues "with the same logic mistake (such as unsafe casting) or the same conceptual mistake ... may be considered duplicates" — [Sherlock docs: Criteria for Issue Validity](https://docs.sherlock.xyz/audits/judging/guidelines)
- Code4rena awarding included 10% "hunter" and "gatherer" bonuses; lifetime "$20M+ paid to wardens"; "16,600+ registered wardens" — [Code4rena docs: Awarding](https://docs.code4rena.com/awarding); [m3dython Code4rena review 2026](https://m3dython.com/blog/code4rena-review-2026)
- Participation: "Wardens (100–600+ per contest) submit findings over a 3–14 day window"; Code4rena claimed "an average of 100+ security researchers participating in each audit" — [m3dython Code4rena review 2026](https://m3dython.com/blog/code4rena-review-2026); [Code4rena site](https://code4rena.com/)
- Historical comparison: in 2021 Code4rena averaged 10 wardens per contest, ~25 by end-2021, up to 59 by May 2022 — [cmichel, Code4rena First $1M stats (May 2022)](https://cmichel.io/code4rena-first-1m-stats/). ### Inferences
- Because the pot is fixed and only H/M count, a newcomer's expected value per contest ≈ (probability of finding a valid H/M that fewer than ~5 others also found) × (pot share). With 100–600 entrants and duplicate decay of ~55% per extra finder, "obvious" bugs pay almost nothing; only unique or near-unique findings pay meaningfully.
- The only published newcomer numbers point to first payouts in the hundreds of dollars (e.g., $445.93 for a 2nd place in a small CodeHawks contest) after months of study; $5k–$30k/year "active participant" figures come from training-course marketing, not audited data.
- With Code4rena gone (May 2026), the crypto contest market is now Cantina (largest pots, most senior field), Sherlock, CodeHawks (beginner tier), and Immunefi competitions (which reserve 20% of pots for pre-selected All Stars).

### Gaps
- No platform publishes what fraction of contest participants receive any payout, or the median payout per participant. The "three out of four findings earn nothing" figure is a practitioner's analysis whose methodology I could not read (dev.to blocked).
- Code4rena's 2025 total awards and Sherlock's annual totals were not found.
- Savant's 6th-place prize amount and Sherlock top-Watson annual earnings not found.
- Could not read any 2025–2026 first-6–12-months earnings write-up in full; only the 2023 milotruck account has full-year numbers.

## Key Question 3: Skills actually required, time to first paid finding, and whether AI tools changed newcomer odds or flooded programs

### Takeaway
Published roadmaps put the first contest at 6–9 months of dedicated study and first meaningful payouts later than that; the skills that pay are protocol-specific reasoning (accounting invariants, share/asset math, access control) plus the ability to write a runnable PoC. AI has shifted the field in both directions: autonomous systems (XBOW, Savant, Anthropic's Mythos program) are now credible finders, but the dominant effect on open programs is a flood of unverified reports — curl's valid rate fell below 5% and it ended its bounty on Feb 1, 2026; Bugcrowd submissions quadrupled in three weeks in March 2026; Immunefi bans AI-generated reports and rate-limits new accounts to one submission per day.

### Cited Findings

**Skills and timeline**
- "A realistic timeline involves Months 1–3 for blockchain fundamentals and Solidity, Months 6–9 entering your first Code4rena or Sherlock contest, probably finishing mid-table or lower" — [DeepStrike roadmap 2025](https://deepstrike.io/blog/smart-contract-auditor-roadmap-2025)
- Example timelines from practitioners: first Solidity April 2023 → first contest July 2023 → full-time Sept 2023 — [AuditOne](https://www.auditone.io/blog-posts/smart-contract-auditor-journey); first Sherlock contest July 2024 → first contest win Jan 2025 — [Sherlock blog](https://sherlock.xyz/post/5-steps-to-becoming-a-top-level-smart-contract-auditor)
- "rounding and precision bugs in share/asset math show up constantly in Sherlock audit contests, and these are considered serious issues by judges" — [Sherlock docs: Criteria for Issue Validity](https://docs.sherlock.xyz/audits/judging/guidelines)
- Immunefi's own "first day" guidance and rules stress PoC quality: ChatGPT "is incapable of smart contract technical analysis and building proper Proofs of Concept" — [Immunefi blog: Your First Day As A Bug Bounty Hunter](https://immunefi.com/blog/all/first-day-bug-bounty-hunter/); [The Block, Immunefi spammed by ChatGPT reports (2023)](https://www.theblock.co/post/240758/immunefi-chatgpt-generated-web3-bug-bounty-reports)

- The AI systems that succeed (XBOW ~1,000 reports/quarter, Anthropic's 2,300 disclosures with Trail of Bits triage) run at industrial scale with human validation layers; a solo operator with Claude Code is not in that regime and competes on the same terms as other humans, plus the "first paid report" handicap.

## Key Question 4: Math-heavy bug classes — paid examples involving cryptography, ZK soundness, precision/rounding, oracle math, and what they paid

### Takeaway
Rounding/precision bugs are the single best-documented math-heavy class that pays: The Graph ($290,497, Jan 2024), DFX Finance ($100k), Balancer ($250k and $1M rounding errors), and a $1M cross-chain integer-truncation bug (Moonbeam/Astar/Acala, June 2023). ## Key Question 5: Legal/ethical guardrails — authorized vs illegal, and how payouts get invalidated

### Takeaway
Authorized work means testing only assets listed in a program's scope, on forks/local environments unless the program explicitly permits otherwise, and submitting privately before any disclosure; testing on mainnet without permission is a permanent-ban offense on Immunefi and potentially a crime. Payouts are routinely reduced or denied for out-of-scope findings, "No Fix, No Pay," KYC failure, disputed severity, or the project simply not paying (a community "Wall of Shame" tracks ~$2.5M unpaid). The Mango Markets ruling (May 2025, under appeal) shows US fraud law's reach over DeFi "exploits" is unsettled, but that is no safe harbor for individuals.

### Cited Findings

**What is authorized**
- Immunefi rules: users agree not to do "Any testing with mainnet or public testnet contracts other than as approved by the applicable Bug Bounty Program"; "testing on mainnet or public testnet is grounds for an immediate and permanent ban" — [Immunefi Rules](https://immunefi.com/rules/); [Immunefi program page example](https://immunefi.com/bug-bounty/immutable/information/)
- SEAL Whitehat Safe Harbor: "a legal framework developed by the Security Alliance (SEAL) for protocols to empower whitehat security researchers to rescue protocol funds during a blackhat attack and redirect those funds back to a protocol-controlled Vault"; "The Safe Harbor Agreement only covers cases of active exploitation by blackhat hackers, and expressly forbids all other hacking activity"; rescued funds must go to official recovery addresses "within 72 hours"; bounties are "enforceable and pre-defined" — [Immunefi Safe Harbor](https://immunefi.com/safe-harbor/); [SEAL Safe Harbor overview](https://frameworks.securityalliance.org/safe-harbor/overview/); [SEAL whitehat page](https://frameworks.securityalliance.org/safe-harbor/whitehat/)
- Adoption: SEAL named 29 companies as "Safe Harbor Champions 2025"; adopted by Uniswap, Balancer and protocols with "$68B+ TVL" — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/white-hat-hackers-saved-billions-crypto-seal-safe-harbor); [Compound governance proposal](https://www.comp.xyz/t/immunefis-safe-harbor-for-compounds-bounty-program/6199)
- Responsible publication: Immunefi requires following its Responsible Publication Policy before public write-ups — [Immunefi Responsible Publication](https://immunefi.com/responsible-publication/)

**How payouts get invalidated or reduced**
- KYC/AML: "You may be required to provide personal information to satisfy Know Your Customer (KYC) and/or anti-money laundering (AML) legal requirements ... at the sole discretion of the Immunefi customer ... Failure to comply ... will result in you being ineligible" — [Immunefi Rules](https://immunefi.com/rules/)
- "No Fix, No Pay": projects that acknowledge but do not fix a vulnerability are not obligated to pay — [Immunefi support: No Fix, No Pay](https://immunefisupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/22617181023889-No-Fix-No-Pay); critique that this "creates a perverse incentive" — [m3dython Immunefi review 2026](https://m3dython.com/blog/immunefi-review-2026)
- Scope disputes: Nov 12, 2024, Trust Security reported a "critical theft-of-funds vulnerability on a forked mainnet"; the project called it out-of-scope; Immunefi said it "does not consider Trust's finding as a valid vulnerability because it would require a user's unintended action to explicitly initiate an infinite approval"; a "tiny goodwill bounty" was offered; Immunefi then suspended Trust Security for 90 days for going public — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/immunefi-suspends-trustsec-over-bounty-payment-dispute); [CoinJournal](https://coinjournal.net/news/immunefi-suspends-trust-security-trustsec-amid-bug-bounty-dispute/)
- Dispute mechanisms: Immunefi mediation, then "Arbitration, the world's first legally binding dispute resolution system for bug bounties," with the London Chamber of Arbitration and Mediation (LCAM), "globally enforceable rulings in 172 countries" — [Immunefi Arbitration (Medium)](https://medium.com/immunefi/immunefi-arbitration-a-new-era-for-onchain-dispute-resolution-bc24af9bdafa); [Immunefi support: Mediation](https://immunefisupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/16752656489873-Mediation); [Arbitration Overview](https://immunefisupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/26081514838673-Arbitration-Overview)
- Non-payment: the community-maintained "Bug Bounty Wall of Shame ... documents nearly $2.5 million in unpaid bounties across dozens of projects"; example: the Arbitrum 2022 bug (exposure "over $680 million") had a "$2 million" advertised reward but the whitehat "was awarded just $780,000" — [Bug Bounty Wall of Shame](https://bug-bounty-wall-of-shame.github.io/); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/a-silent-security-scandal-defi-bug-bounty-wall-of-shame-shows-millions-of-unpaid-bounties/)
- Injective: researcher "al_f4lc0n" accused Injective of ghosting "for three months, despite fixing the bug, and later lowballing the bounty" after a disclosure the researcher framed as putting ~$500M at risk — [Protos](https://protos.com/whitehat-hacker-accuses-injective-of-ghosting-after-500m-bug-disclosure/)
- AI-generated submissions "will result in a ban" — [Immunefi on X](https://x.com/immunefi/status/1600889765448273920); Oct 2025 submission caps for unpaid accounts (1 per 24h) — [Immunefi support](https://immunefisupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/39568715723409-Updated-Submission-Rules)
- Standardized competition reward terms exist (Immunefi) — [Immunefi support: Standardized Competition Reward Terms](https://immunefisupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/31657285001873-Standardized-Competition-Reward-Terms) (not readable)

**Where the legal line sits**
- Mango Markets: on May 23, 2025, Judge Arun Subramanian vacated Avraham Eisenberg's April 2024 convictions (commodities fraud, manipulation, wire fraud) on improper venue and because "the automated nature of Mango Markets' smart contract meant that Eisenberg could not have made 'false representations'"; prosecutors appealed and the appeal "remained active in 2026"; Eisenberg remains imprisoned on an unrelated CSAM conviction — [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/breaking-federal-judge-overturns-all-criminal-convictions-in-mango-markets-case-against-avraham-eisenberg); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/322183/mango-markets-attacker-conviction-overturned-why); [DL News, appeal](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/prosecutors-appeal-acquittal-of-mango-markets-exploiter/); [Bloomberg, 2025-05-24](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-24/crypto-trader-s-convictions-vacated-in-mango-markets-fraud-case)
- Malta: two university students were arrested and charged for responsibly disclosing a vulnerability in a student app "and suggesting a bug bounty"; pardoned by parliamentary vote in March 2025 — [Dedaub, Strengthening Legal Protections for White Hat Hackers](https://dedaub.com/blog/white-hat-hackers/)
- Post-exploit "10% whitehat bounty" deals (GMX July 2025; Renegade May 2026) are negotiated with attackers under threat of "civil or criminal action" — [Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/05/11/renegade-whitehat-crypto-recovery/); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/07/09/decentralized-exchange-gmx-exploited-for-usd42m-offers-hacker-10-white-hat-bounty)

### Inferences
- Practically: (1) only touch code that is explicitly in scope on a live program page; (2) reproduce on a local fork, never mainnet; (3) submit through the platform before any public mention; (4) expect KYC; (5) expect the project, not the platform, to decide severity and whether to pay, with mediation/arbitration as the only recourse.
- Even a valid critical can be paid at a fraction of the advertised cap (Arbitrum $780k of $2M) or not at all (Wall of Shame). Advertised maximums are ceilings, not expectations.
- "Exploit first, negotiate a 10% bounty later" is not a legal earning path; the Eisenberg ruling turned on venue and the elements of wire fraud, is under appeal, and does not immunize unauthorized fund extraction.

- Immunefi's full Rules, No-Fix-No-Pay and Safe Harbor pages could not be fetched; wording is from search excerpts.

## Cross-cutting assessment for the report writer (inferences, not new citations)

- Is meaningful money realistic for a motivated, AI-fluent non-professional? The evidence says: possible but with a long, front-loaded ramp and a highly skewed payoff distribution. Published newcomer outcomes are first payouts in the hundreds of dollars after 6–12 months; the credible full-time mid-tier figure is ~$170k/year (2023, a top-decile competitor in a smaller field); the top 10 on Immunefi have taken roughly a third of all money ever paid.
- The market got tougher in 2025–2026: Immunefi's average critical fell 45% in 2025; Code4rena closed (May 2026); Immunefi rate-limits unproven accounts and reserves 20% of competition pots for pre-selected All Stars; AI-slop policies treat unproven submitters with suspicion.
- The math edge maps best onto DeFi precision/rounding/accounting bugs ($100k–$1M paid examples), moderately onto ZK soundness ($50k–$450k, with $1M caps but few payouts), and poorly onto cryptanalysis (no bounty market).
- Minimum viable path suggested by the sources: CodeHawks First Flights → Sherlock/Cantina contests focused on one bug class (fixed-point math) → first paid report → Immunefi bounties on programs with documented payouts, always on local forks and within scope.
