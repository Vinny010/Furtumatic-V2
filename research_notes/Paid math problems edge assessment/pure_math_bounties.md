## Key Question 1: Erdős problems — how many open, what bounties remain, who pays, how many paid out 2024–2026, and which were solved with AI (by whom, with what background)?

### Takeaway
Roughly 100 of ~1,200 catalogued Erdős problems carry a cash prize, with unsolved prize value on the order of $39k total (mostly $25–$1,000 items; a few $500–$10,000). Prizes are now paid by the Combinatorics Foundation (Iowa nonprofit, administered by Steve Butler) only after journal publication, and historically only a handful have ever been paid. 2025–2026 saw a documented wave of AI-assisted solutions (GPT-5.2/5.4/5.5 Pro, Harmonic's Aristotle, DeepMind provers, OpenAI internal models incl. "Astra", Claude), verified by Terence Tao and others and mostly Lean-formalized; the most famous non-specialist involvement is 23-year-old Liam Price (no advanced math training) whose single GPT-5.4 Pro prompt produced the proof of #1196 in April 2026 — but I found **no source documenting any cash payout to any AI-assisted solver** as of Sept 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Scale of the problem set and the prize pool**
- As of a September 2026 search summary, the Erdős problems database holds 1,221 problems of which 586 (48%) are solved, leaving ~635 open — [Epoch AI, "Announcing FrontierMath Erdős"](https://epoch.ai/latest/announcing-frontiermath-erdos) (via search summary; same summary references [erdosproblems.com](https://www.erdosproblems.com/))
- Epoch AI's FrontierMath Erdős benchmark is a curated set of 68 "significant" Erdős problems open as of August 2026, selected by Thomas Bloom and formalized in Lean — [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/latest/announcing-frontiermath-erdos)
- Over 2024 and the first eight months of 2025, 111 problems on the Erdős list changed status from "open" to "solved" — [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/latest/announcing-frontiermath-erdos) (via search summary)
- Only approximately 100 problems on erdosproblems.com are known to have monetary prizes; the prizes page states "the value of unsolved problems is currently $38,670" — [erdosproblems.com/prizes](https://www.erdosproblems.com/prizes) (via search summary)
- An earlier snapshot of the same prizes page reported "492 problems solved out of 1103; value of solved problems $21,063 out of $59,733 total (35%)" — [erdosproblems.com/prizes](https://www.erdosproblems.com/prizes) (via search summary; snapshot date unknown — see Gaps)
- Erdős's prizes ranged from $25 for problems he considered "not particularly difficult but still tricky" up to $10,000 for what he called "a hopeless problem" in number theory — [Scott W. Williams, "Million Buck Problems"](https://www.math.buffalo.edu/~sww/0papers/million.buck.problems.mi.pdf)
- The unit-distance conjecture (problem #90) carried a $500 Erdős prize, "first offered in print in 1995 for a proof or disproof of the upper bound ≤ n^(1+o(1))" — [arXiv 2605.20695, "Remarks on the disproof of the unit distance conjecture"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20695) (via search summary)

**Who pays now, and the payout rules**
- Erdős prizes "are now awarded by the Combinatorics Foundation, administered by Steve Butler"; the Foundation "will honor the rewards that Paul had offered, within reason" — [erdosproblems.com FAQ](https://www.erdosproblems.com/faq) and [Combinatorics Foundation, Erdős problems page](https://www.combinatoricsfoundation.org/erd%C5%91s-problems) (via search summaries)
- Conditions to claim: "Prizes will only be awarded after the publication of a solution in a reputable journal, with accompanying documentation verifying that Erdős offered that amount"; "the reward must be documented in a publication of Paul's (no second-hand rumors, lectures, or other sources) and the solution must be published in a reputable, peer-reviewed journal in mathematics" — [Combinatorics Foundation](https://www.combinatoricsfoundation.org/erd%C5%91s-problems) (via search summary)
- The teorth/erdosproblems wiki (read directly) confirms Erdős prizes are "currently administered by Steve Butler" but gives no submission procedure or success rate — [teorth/erdosproblems wiki, "What to do when I think I managed to get AI to solve an Erdős problem?"](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/What-to-do-when-I-think-I-managed-to-get-AI-to-solve-an-Erd%C5%91s-problem%3F)
- Historically, Ronald Graham managed Erdős's finances (Erdős never had a checking account) and controlled a small fund left by Erdős for paying bounties; Graham personally contributed $5,000 toward a $10,000 prize — [Quanta, "Cash for Math: The Erdős Prizes Live On" (June 2017)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/cash-for-math-the-erdos-prizes-live-on-20170605/) (via search summary)

**Historical payouts (actual money, actual recipients)**
- 1974: Erdős paid $1,000 to Endre Szemerédi (first major payout) — [Quanta 2017](https://www.quantamagazine.org/cash-for-math-the-erdos-prizes-live-on-20170605/)
- The $10,000 prime-gaps prize was paid (from Erdős's surviving funds plus $5,000 from Graham) to James Maynard, Ben Green, Kevin Ford, Sergei Konyagin and Terence Tao — [Quanta 2017](https://www.quantamagazine.org/cash-for-math-the-erdos-prizes-live-on-20170605/) (via search summary). All five are professional research mathematicians (Tao and Maynard are Fields Medalists).
- Between 1998 (when Graham and Chung published their book of ~200 graph-theory problems) and 2017, Graham and Chung "had to pay off on only three Erdős prizes from the fund" — [Quanta 2017](https://www.quantamagazine.org/cash-for-math-the-erdos-prizes-live-on-20170605/) (via search summary)
- The happy-ending problem carries a $500 bounty — [Quanta 2017](https://www.quantamagazine.org/cash-for-math-the-erdos-prizes-live-on-20170605/)

**AI-assisted solutions, 2025–2026 (from the teorth wiki, read directly; data frozen 30 June 2026)**
- The wiki classifies AI contributions as 1(a) AI standalone, 1(b) AI alongside literature, 1(c) AI building on known literature, 1(d) AI collaborating with humans, plus secondary roles 2(a) literature search, 2(b) formalization, 2(c) rewriting, 2(d) computation. It states explicitly that it "is not a benchmark," has "significant selection bias," and that featured problems "may reflect obscurity rather than difficulty" — [teorth/erdosproblems wiki, AI contributions](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems)

- The wiki's "Notable cases" page lists four: #1026 (Dec 2025), #728 (Jan 2026, "first autonomous and nontrivial solution to an Erdős problem by AI systems"; general result also solving #729 and #401), #1196 (Apr 2026; technique also resolved #1217, #164, two further conjectures and inspired #858), and #90 (May 2026; also disproved #92) — [teorth wiki, Notable cases](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/Notable-cases-of-AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems)

**Problem #728 (first "autonomous" AI solve) — who did it**
- On 4 January 2026 GPT-5.2 Pro, "prompted by Liam Price and Kevin Barreto," produced an argument for #728; Harmonic's Aristotle turned it into a Lean proof posted 6 January 2026; Boris Alexeev produced/simplified the Lean file (108 KB, Lean v4.24.0); Nat Sothanaphan wrote it up as arXiv 2601.07421 — [adamghaida/ai-hall-of-fame PR #43](https://github.com/adamghaida/ai-hall-of-fame/pull/43) and [arXiv 2601.07421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07421) (via search summaries)
- Tao called it the "most unambiguous" instance of AI solving an open problem, but warned the result "says more about speed than difficulty" — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/terence-tao-says-gpt-5-2-pro-cracked-an-erdos-problem-but-warns-the-win-says-more-about-speed-than-difficulty/); [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/yet-another-erdos-problem-solved-with-help-of-gpt-5-2-terrance-tao-calls-it-most-unambiguous-instance-of-ai-solving-an-open-problem/) (via search summaries)
- Three Erdős problems were solved with AI assistance in a single seven-day period in January 2026, all verified by Tao — [Delante AI News, Jan 2026](https://delante.co/gpt-5-2-solves-erdos-problems/) (via search summary; secondary source)

**Problem #1196 (the "amateur" case) — background of solvers**
- Liam Price is "a 23-year-old with no advanced mathematics training"; his collaborator Kevin Barreto is "a second-year undergraduate at Cambridge." They "jump-started the AI-for-Erdős craze by prompting ChatGPT with open problems from the Erdős problems website," and "an AI researcher gift[ed] them ChatGPT Pro subscriptions" — [Scientific American, "Amateur armed with ChatGPT 'vibe maths' a 60-year-old problem"](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/amateur-armed-with-chatgpt-vibe-maths-a-60-year-old-problem/) (via search summary)
- Price entered #1196 (primitive sets; open since 1965) as a single prompt to GPT-5.4 Pro on 13 April 2026; the model reasoned ~80 minutes and produced a proof using Markov chains with von Mangoldt weights, a route no human had tried — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/amateur-armed-with-chatgpt-vibe-maths-a-60-year-old-problem/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openais-gpt-5-4-pro-reportedly-solves-a-longstanding-open-erdos-math-problem-in-under-two-hours/) (via search summaries)
- erdosproblems.com marks #1196 "PROVED," credited to "GPT-5.4 Pro (prompted by Liam Price)"; Tao validated it and called it a "meaningful contribution" — [erdosproblems.com/1196](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1196); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/amateur-armed-with-chatgpt-vibe-maths-a-60-year-old-problem/) (via search summaries)
- The resulting May 2026 paper (arXiv 2605.00301) is by Alexeev, Barreto, Li, Lichtman, Price, Shah, Tang and Tao — i.e., the amateur's result was written up jointly with professional mathematicians including Tao and Jared Duker Lichtman (Stanford) — [TheJustinSunPrize/awards PR #1074](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/pull/1074) (read directly); [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/latest/announcing-frontiermath-erdos) (via search summary)

**OpenAI's internal-model results (not available to the public)**
- 20 May 2026: OpenAI announced an internal, non-public model had found a counterexample to Erdős's 1946 unit-distance conjecture (#90), "providing an infinite family of examples that yield a polynomial improvement"; Quanta called it "the first historically significant proof to come from an AI model"; nine mathematicians examined the disproof line by line and found it correct; human mathematicians "would substantially improve on it within weeks" — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/model-disproves-discrete-geometry-conjecture/); [Quanta, "Why the Legendary Erdős Problems Are Falling to AI" (3 Aug 2026)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/why-the-legendary-erdos-problems-are-falling-to-ai-20260803/) (via search summaries)
- 1 August 2026: OpenAI announced an unreleased model codenamed "Astra" produced ten results on problems open ≥10 years, including Erdős #146 and #180 (disproofs of the Erdős–Simonovits compactness conjecture and an Erdős degeneracy conjecture) and #183 (superexponential lower bound for multicolor triangle Ramsey numbers), each with a Lean 4 certificate; OpenAI said the tokens would cost roughly $2,000 at API rates — [OpenAI, "Ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science"](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/); [Zvi Mowshowitz, 3 Aug 2026](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/08/03/openais-unreleased-model-astra-solves-ten-major-open-mathematics-problems/) (via search summaries)

### Inferences
- The only widely documented case of a genuine non-specialist being credited on a solved Erdős problem is Liam Price (#728 with Barreto, then #1196). In both cases the "solving" was prompting a frontier model, and the acceptance path ran through professional mathematicians (Tao et al.) who verified, wrote up, and co-authored the paper, plus a Lean formalization by others (Alexeev, Aristotle).
- Because the Combinatorics Foundation pays only after peer-reviewed journal publication and only for prizes documented in Erdős's own papers, and because most AI-solved problems listed on the wiki are not stated to carry prizes, the realistic cash from the 2025–2026 wave is small: the largest AI-solved prize problem I found is #90 ($500), and I found no record it was paid.
- The problems that fell to AI were disproportionately obscure/never-seriously-studied rather than famous prize problems (the wiki says so explicitly), so a non-specialist's edge comes from breadth-of-search over neglected problems, not from cracking the well-known $500–$10,000 items.

### Gaps
- I could not read erdosproblems.com/prizes directly; the two count snapshots ($38,670 unsolved; 492/1103 solved, $21,063/$59,733) are from different dates and I cannot date either precisely. The exact number of prize-bearing problems (~100) and the per-amount distribution ($25/$50/$100/$250/$500/$1,000/$5,000/$10,000 counts) could not be verified.
- I found no source listing any Erdős prize actually paid out by the Combinatorics Foundation in 2024, 2025 or 2026, nor any statement that OpenAI (for #90, $500) or Price/Barreto received money. Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- Whether #728, #1196, #1026, #205, #457, #125, #38, #846, #146, #180, #183 carried any Erdős prize is not stated in the sources I could access.
- Backgrounds of Nat Sothanaphan, Boris Alexeev, Aron Bhalla, Enrique Barschkis, Wouter van Doorn and Bartosz Naskrecki were not established from sources reached.

## Key Question 2: Are there Erdős problems experts consider tractable/low-hanging, and what does it take to get a solution accepted?

### Takeaway
Tao estimates only ~1–2% of open Erdős problems are easy enough for current AI to solve with minimal human help, and an independent database of AI attempts found roughly the same success rate concentrated on easier problems. Acceptance on erdosproblems.com is informal (Bloom/Tao and forum review, strongly aided by a sorry-free Lean proof), but a cash prize additionally requires publication in a reputable peer-reviewed journal.

### Cited Findings
- Tao estimates "only around one to two percent of currently open Erdős problems are simple enough for today's AI tools to solve with minimal human help"; Erdős problems vary in difficulty "by several orders of magnitude," and "many of the easier ones have never been seriously studied" — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/terence-tao-says-gpt-5-2-pro-cracked-an-erdos-problem-but-warns-the-win-says-more-about-speed-than-difficulty/) (via search summary; Tao's original comments were on Mathstodon, Jan 2026)
- A database by Paata Ivanisvili and Mehmet Mars Seven tracking AI attempts at Erdős problems "show[s] actual success rates of just one to two percent, clustered around easier problems" — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/gpt-5-2-pro-solves-another-erdos-problem-while-a-new-database-reveals-most-attempts-still-fail/); [arXiv 2601.22401](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22401v2) (via search summaries)
- erdosproblems.com forum rules: "AI assistance in generating ideas or helping to formulate the text of a comment is allowed, but should be disclosed. The contents of all comments, including any mathematical claims, should be independently verified by a human before posting here. If you do not understand the mathematics yourself, please do not post it here." The site "does not exist to serve as a benchmark for AI progress, or for people to request feedback on their AI-generated proofs if they do not understand them themselves" — [erdosproblems.com forum, "AI Contributions" thread](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/AI%20Contributions?order=oldest) (via search summary)
- Forum rule on Lean: post "only once you have either (a) understood and verified the mathematics yourself, or (b) obtained a sorry and bug-free Lean formalisation of the proof" — [erdosproblems.com forum](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/AI%20Contributions%202) (via search summary)
- The teorth wiki's step-by-step guidance (read directly): (1) self-assess — "If you cannot communicate the main ideas of the proof to others without consulting the AI solution, this is a red flag"; (2) literature review via Google Scholar/MathSciNet/zbMath; (3) ask AI for criticism because current systems tend toward "sycophancy"; (4) formalize in Lean — "a valid Lean certificate will significantly increase the chance that your solution receives expert attention"; (5) write to academic standards; (6) post to the forum. Red flags: solutions that are "suspiciously short" or elementary, proofs that don't use all hypotheses, misformalization of the problem — [teorth wiki](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/What-to-do-when-I-think-I-managed-to-get-AI-to-solve-an-Erd%C5%91s-problem%3F)
- Google DeepMind's formal-conjectures repository tracks Erdős problem statements in Lean and marks problems solved via PRs (e.g., #260) — [google-deepmind/formal-conjectures PR #4446](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/4446) (via search summary)
- Prize condition (repeated for emphasis): "the solution must be published in a reputable, peer-reviewed journal in mathematics" and the reward "must be documented in a publication of Paul's" — [Combinatorics Foundation](https://www.combinatoricsfoundation.org/erd%C5%91s-problems) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- "Solved on the website" and "eligible for the bounty" are different bars. Price's #1196 proof was accepted on-site within about a week because Tao personally validated it; the cash bar (journal publication) takes months to years and requires a co-author or referee community willing to engage.
- The tractable 1–2% tail is exactly what frontier labs (OpenAI, DeepMind, Harmonic, Math Inc.) and Tao's collaborators have been systematically sweeping since late 2025 with non-public models and large compute; a solo non-specialist with consumer-tier tools is competing against that sweep for the same low-hanging fruit.

### Gaps
- No source gave a curated list of "tractable" open prize problems; the tractability estimate is a percentage, not a list.
- I could not access Tao's original Mathstodon posts or blog posts (terrytao.wordpress.com not fetched) to confirm exact wording of the 1–2% estimate.

## Key Question 3: Millennium Prize — status of the six unsolved problems, the OpenAI Navier–Stokes claim, and Clay's rules

### Takeaway
As of September 2026 Clay still lists all six remaining problems as unsolved. On 8 September 2026 OpenAI announced a Lean-formalized proof of finite-time blow-up for the 3D Navier–Stokes equations with smooth external forcing (Clay's options C/D), stated it will not claim the $1M, and was immediately embroiled in a priority dispute with NYU's Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic's Levent Alpöge. Clay's rules require publication in a refereed journal plus two years of general acceptance before any award, so no prize can be awarded before ~2028 at the earliest even if the claim holds.

### Cited Findings
- Clay rules: "Before consideration, a proposed solution must be published in a refereed mathematics journal of world-wide repute, and it must also have general acceptance in the mathematics community two years after that publication." After that, the Scientific Advisory Board decides whether it merits detailed consideration and, if so, constitutes a special advisory committee "which will include at least one SAB member and at least two non-SAB members who are experts in the area" — [Clay Mathematics Institute, Rules for the Millennium Prize Problems](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/rules/); [Clay rules PDF](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/millennium_prize_rules_0.pdf) (via search summaries)
- Poincaré is the only problem officially declared solved; Clay awarded the prize to Grigori Perelman in 2010 and he declined it (on 1 July 2010), because it was not also offered to Richard Hamilton — [Wikipedia, Millennium Prize Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Prize_Problems); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/science/russian-math-genius-may-reject-1m-award-1.915054) (via search summaries)
- The six remaining problems (Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer, Hodge, Navier–Stokes, P vs NP, Riemann, Yang–Mills) "continue to carry the $1 million prize offer" as of 2026 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Prize_Problems) (via search summary)
- 8 September 2026: OpenAI announced its system produced a proof that a finite-time singularity occurs in the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, with formal verification in Lean; the run reportedly involved ~10,000 AI agents over roughly 88 hours — [OpenAI, "On the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem"](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/); [CNBC, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/openai-navier-stokes-math-problem-solved.html); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02842-5) (via search summaries)
- The claim covers blow-up under smooth external forcing — Clay options C and D, which are "formally part of the Clay problem as written" — while "many mathematicians consider the unforced options (A and B) to be the deeper question, and those remain definitively unresolved"; "the proof relies on a smooth external force, a route the written problem permits but that most working mathematicians exclude from the question they care about" — [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/openai-navier-stokes-math-problem); [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/openai-navier-stokes-millennium-prize) (via search summaries)
- Clay's response: "The Clay Mathematics Institute has not accepted the result and still lists Navier-Stokes among its unsolved Millennium Prize problems" — [Implicator.ai, "Clay Institute Won't Call Navier-Stokes Solved by OpenAI"](https://www.implicator.ai/clay-institute-navier-stokes-openai-proof-claim/) (via search summary). A separate secondary source says that as of 11 September Clay "acknowledged the problem 'could have been settled' and opened its formal verification process — a step short of certification, with at least 2 years of public review still required" — [Seoul Inside Substack, 12 Sep 2026](https://seoulinside.substack.com/p/did-openai-just-solve-navierstokes) (lower-reliability secondary source; wording not confirmed against Clay's own site)
- OpenAI "stated in its announcement that it would not claim the $1 million Clay Millennium Prize," saying "its primary objective was to demonstrate the capabilities of large-scale agentic reasoning rather than seek monetary rewards" — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02842-5); [KuCoin blog](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/en-did-ai-just-solve-the-1-million-navier-stokes-problem-what-openai-s-proof-actually-means) (via search summaries)
- Priority dispute: ~12 hours before OpenAI's announcement, NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster published a statement on his AI-assisted work with Levent Alpöge (a mathematician at Anthropic); Buckmaster noted OpenAI found its solution "in the past few days" after becoming aware of his work; Buckmaster and Alpöge accused OpenAI of using private chat logs and uploaded drafts to guide its agents; OpenAI denied this — [Axios, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/08/openai-math-solution-navier-stokes-credit); [MIT Technology Review, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/08/1143747/what-openais-latest-controversy-tells-us-about-the-future-of-math/); [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/b05bc0db-1896-4282-9106-fb3da78adc03) (via search summaries)
- A June 2026 arXiv preprint, "Blow-up and uniqueness of Leray-Hopf solutions to forced Navier-Stokes equations" (arXiv 2606.15189), predates OpenAI's announcement and addresses the forced variant — [arXiv 2606.15189](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15189) (via search result title only; authors not confirmed)
- A separate rumor that OpenAI had solved the Hodge conjecture circulated on social media in 2026 and drew criticism from mathematicians; I found no OpenAI announcement or verification — [Vocal Media, "The Hodge Conjecture Rumor"](https://vocal.media/fyi/the-hodge-conjecture-rumor-openai-a-viral-tweet-and-the-math-worlds-quiet-fury) (low-reliability source; treat as rumor only)

### Inferences
- Even in the best case for OpenAI, the two-year clock only starts after journal publication, which has not happened; the earliest possible award is ~2028–2029, and OpenAI has said it will not claim the money anyway. There is no path by which an individual claims this prize on the back of OpenAI's result.
- The forced-vs-unforced distinction means the community may treat the "real" Navier–Stokes problem as still open even if Clay eventually accepts C/D — the same pattern as the Erdős cases, where "solved as written" and "solved as intended" diverge.

### Gaps
- I could not read Clay's own statement text (claymath.org blocked). The "could have been settled" wording is from a Substack and should be verified before quoting.
- Authors and content of arXiv 2606.15189 were not confirmed.
- No independent peer review of OpenAI's Lean formalization was found as of 21 Sept 2026.

## Key Question 4: Beal Conjecture ($1M via AMS) and Collatz (Bakuage ¥120M)

### Takeaway
Both prizes are standing and unclaimed. Beal requires publication in a refereed journal that the AMS-appointed committee deems of the "highest editorial standards"; Bakuage requires publication in a journal "generally accepted by the global Mathematical Society" and does not accept direct submissions. Neither has credible verified progress toward a full solution; Collatz has several 2026 AI-assisted preprints/claims, none verified.

### Cited Findings

**Beal Prize**
- The Beal Prize Fund is held "as a restricted asset of the AMS, with US$1,000,000 to be awarded if the Beal Prize Conjecture is proved or a counterexample is presented"; the work "must be published in a refereed mathematics publication which is respected and, in the opinion of the Beal Prize Committee (BPC), maintains the highest editorial standards" — [AMS, Beal Prize Rules and Procedures](https://www.ams.org/profession/prizes-awards/ams-supported/beal-prize-rules) (via search summary)
- Andrew Beal raised the prize to $1 million in 2013 — [Nature blogs, "Mathematics prize ups the ante to $1 million"](https://blogs.nature.com/blog/mathematics-prize-ups-the-ante-to-1-million/); [Business Standard, June 2013](https://www.business-standard.com/article/beyond-business/1-million-reward-to-solve-this-math-problem-113060500302_1.html) (via search summaries)
- As of September 2026 the prize "remains unclaimed" — [search summary of AMS/MathWorld sources](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/MathematicsPrizes.html)

**Bakuage Collatz Prize**
- Announced 7 July 2021 by Bakuage Co., Ltd. (Shibuya, Tokyo): ¥120,000,000 (≈US$1,085,000 at ¥110.50/$ on 29 June 2021) "to anyone who has revealed the truth of the Collatz conjecture" — [PR Newswire, 7 Jul 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bakuage-offers-prize-of-120-million-jpy-to-whoever-solves-collatz-conjecture-math-problem-unsolved-for-84-years-301326629.html)
- Rules: research papers must "specify the Solution Method" and be published in an "Appropriate Publication (excluding advertisements and nonacademic research publications)"; accepted journals are "those that have been generally accepted by the global Mathematical Society"; the company does "not take random submissions over the Internet"; where press release and rules conflict, the rules on the prize site prevail — [Bakuage prize rules PDF (7 Jul 2021)](https://mathprize.net/files/collatz-conjecture-rule-en-20210707.pdf); [mathprize.net](https://mathprize.net/posts/collatz-conjecture/) (via search summaries)
- Collatz has been verified for all positive integers up to 2.36×10^21; no general proof exists — [Wikipedia, Collatz conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collatz_conjecture) (via search summary)
- 2026 claims: an "Independent Researcher Yoshiki Ueoka, the AI Gemini, and two collaborators" claim a complete proof via "Collatz Phase Representation" (Medium post; unverified); a human–LLM "quantitative framework" preprint (arXiv 2603.11066, revised through March 2026) proves structural results and reduces to "a single diagnosed open hypothesis" (i.e., not a proof); a ProofAtlas AI result shows a positive-density set reaching 1 within 10.46 ln(n) steps — [Medium/Ueoka](https://medium.com/@u.yoshiki.phys/breaking-collatz-conjecture-proof-finalized-b722d4af343d); [arXiv 2603.11066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11066v2); [ProofAtlas](https://www.proofatlas.ai/advances/) (via search summaries; none peer-reviewed or accepted)
- A page by Ingo Althöfer catalogs multiple historical Collatz prizes — [althofer.de/collatz-prizes](https://althofer.de/collatz-prizes.html) (blocked; not read)

### Inferences
- Both prizes are structurally gated by journal publication in top venues, which for a problem of this notoriety means the manuscript must survive referees who are inundated with crank submissions. AI-generated "proofs" of Collatz are already appearing on Medium/OSF in 2026 and are not being taken seriously.
- Tao's 2019 "almost all orbits" result (the strongest known partial result) came from a Fields Medalist; nothing since has been accepted as substantially closer.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether the Beal rules include a waiting period after publication (the search summary said "no specific mention of a 'two years' requirement in the material found"; the AMS page could not be read).
- Bakuage rules: expiry date, claim window, and whether the prize is still funded in 2026 were not verifiable (mathprize.net blocked). No source after 2021 confirms the prize's continued existence, though none reports its withdrawal.
- The Althöfer Collatz-prizes list could not be read.

## Key Question 5: Other current standing math bounties (companies, foundations, individuals)

### Takeaway
The newest and most AI-relevant bounty is the Justin Sun Prize (announced 16 Sept 2026): a GitHub-based, Lean-verified registry of ~1,022 problems (many Erdős problems) paying separately for provers and formalizers, with a top prize of up to $1M reportedly awarded to OpenAI's Navier–Stokes team; but the registry's own confirmed-awards page shows zero awards recorded as of this research. Beyond that, the standing bounties are the Erdős pool (~$39k unsolved), Beal ($1M), Bakuage Collatz (¥120M) and the six Clay prizes ($6M).

### Cited Findings
- Justin Sun Prize announced 16 September 2026 as "a zero-trust, decentralized academic bounty mechanism based on a 'problem list,' with breakthroughs in fundamental disciplines and machine formal verification as the criteria for awarding prizes, and a top prize of up to US$1 million for a single problem." Problem list "includes formal verification of the Poincaré Conjecture, the Riemann Hypothesis, Goldbach's Conjecture, and a large number of unsolved problems proposed during the lifetime of ... Paul Erdős"; "problems ... can be added only, never removed, and awarded prize funds ... do not need to be returned under any circumstances" — [The Block (sponsored), 16 Sep 2026](https://www.theblock.co/news/sponsored/2026-09-16-justin-sun-establishes-the-justin-sun-prize-my-wealth-came-from-mathematics-and-will-return-to-mathematics-415271); [Justin Sun, "Why I Am Establishing the Justin Sun Prize" (X article)](https://x.com/justinsuntron/article/2100132601424232711) (via search summaries)
- Press release: "The first list of Justin Sun Prize winners was announced, recognizing solutions and formalized proofs involving 66 mathematical problems"; "The top prize, worth US$1 million, was awarded to the OpenAI research team in recognition of its solution and formalized proof for the Existence and Smoothness of the Three-Dimensional Navier–Stokes Equations problem"; "the Lean formalization and verification were completed by GPT-6 Astra" — [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/55d72756-f431-47f6-bb03-79090035ec2f); [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/justin-sun-million-dollar-math-prize-openai/) (via search summaries; press-release-derived)
- Registry structure (read directly): "canonical, machine-checkable record"; problem bank of 1,022 problems; separate tracking of confirmed awards vs candidates; 14-day public review period per contribution; maintainers "independently check the statement and reproduce verification before acceptance"; applicants must "apply only for yourself using your own GitHub account; proxy applications and collection for others are not accepted"; prover and formalizer roles can claim independently but "both awards require an accepted mathematical solution and a verified complete Lean proof"; payment details ("payment network/address") sent by email — [TheJustinSunPrize/awards](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards); [docs/award-process.md](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/main/docs/award-process.md)
- **Conflict:** the registry's confirmed-awards page states "No awards are recorded yet. Follow the confirmation and delivery process before publishing an award" — [awards/README.md](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/main/awards/README.md) (read directly, 21 Sep 2026) — contradicting press coverage of a $1M award to OpenAI and "66 problems" of winners.
- Press coverage says "the problem-solving bounty applies only to challenges solved after they are added to the official registry. If a problem is already solved when it is included, the prover's name is still recorded, but the prize goes entirely to whoever formalizes it for the machine" — [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/55d72756-f431-47f6-bb03-79090035ec2f) (via search summary). The award-process doc I read contained no such restriction — another inconsistency.
- Example candidate submissions: JSP-000494 (Erdős #607) "complete Lean formalization and the Szemerédi–Trotter solution" — [PR #1591](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/pull/1591); JSP-001022 (catalogued as Erdős #1217) — a Lean formalization packaged by Qin Zhao from work by gauss-math-inc/Math, Inc. was **withdrawn by the submitter** after an audit showed the proof addressed #1196 rather than the catalogued #1217 — [PR #1074](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/pull/1074) (read directly); JSP-001018 (Erdős #1213) has a community Lean 4 formalization repo — [CN-F90/JSP-001018-Lean](https://github.com/CN-F90/JSP-001018-Lean) (via search summary)
- Per-problem bounty amounts for the Justin Sun Prize were **not found** in any accessed document (see Gaps).
- Other standing bounties confirmed above: Clay ($1M × 6 unsolved), Beal ($1M), Bakuage Collatz (¥120M), Erdős pool (~$38,670 unsolved, per erdosproblems.com). I found no other new corporate or foundation cash bounties for specific open pure-math problems in 2025–2026 searches.

### Inferences
- The Justin Sun Prize is the only bounty whose rules are explicitly built for the AI + Lean workflow and which pays a *formalizer* separately from the *prover*. For a motivated non-specialist, formalizing an already-solved Erdős problem in Lean is a more plausible route to any payout than proving a new one — but as of 21 Sept 2026 the registry has paid nothing and its published rules are inconsistent with its press releases, so this is unproven.
- The registry is already receiving mislabeled/withdrawn submissions within days of launch (PR #1074), suggesting a crowded, low-signal queue.

### Gaps
- Justin Sun Prize: no per-problem amounts, no funding escrow evidence, no payment currency, no confirmed payouts. The claimed $1M to OpenAI is press-release-only and contradicted by the registry.
- The press release's "66 problems" of first winners could not be reconciled with the registry.
- No source found for any XTX/AIMO-style or other corporate pure-math bounty active in 2026 beyond those listed.

## Key Question 6: Historical base rate — how many people without a research-math background have ever collected a math prize of this kind?

### Takeaway
I found **zero documented cases** of a person without a research-mathematics background collecting an Erdős, Clay, Beal or Collatz cash prize. Every documented Erdős payout went to professional mathematicians (Szemerédi; Maynard/Green/Ford/Konyagin/Tao), only three prizes were paid from the Graham fund between 1998 and 2017, the only Millennium award (Perelman, 2010) was declined, and Beal and Bakuage have never paid. The 2026 amateur (Liam Price) is credited on the erdosproblems.com site and on an arXiv paper with Tao, but no payout to him is documented.

### Cited Findings
- Erdős payouts on record: $1,000 to Endre Szemerédi (1974); $10,000 to Maynard, Green, Ford, Konyagin and Tao (prime gaps, paid from Erdős's funds plus $5,000 from Graham); only three prizes paid from the Graham/Chung fund between 1998 and 2017 — [Quanta 2017](https://www.quantamagazine.org/cash-for-math-the-erdos-prizes-live-on-20170605/) (via search summary)
- Perelman was awarded the $1M Millennium Prize for Poincaré in 2010 and rejected it on 1 July 2010 — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/science/russian-math-genius-may-reject-1m-award-1.915054); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigori_Perelman) (via search summaries)
- Beal Prize: unclaimed since inception (raised to $1M in 2013) — [AMS rules](https://www.ams.org/profession/prizes-awards/ams-supported/beal-prize-rules) (via search summary)
- Bakuage Collatz: no accepted claim reported since 2021 — [PR Newswire 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bakuage-offers-prize-of-120-million-jpy-to-whoever-solves-collatz-conjecture-math-problem-unsolved-for-84-years-301326629.html) and absence of any later report
- Liam Price (23, "no advanced mathematics training," "nor was he a researcher with a PhD") is credited for #1196 on erdosproblems.com; verification was by Tao; the paper is co-authored with Tao, Lichtman and others — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/amateur-armed-with-chatgpt-vibe-maths-a-60-year-old-problem/); [gipyeong-lee.github.io summary](https://gipyeong-lee.github.io/2026/05/06/Amateur-armed-with-ChatGPT-solves-an-Erds-problem.en/); [PR #1074](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/pull/1074) (via search summaries / direct read). No source reports a cash payment to Price.
- Journal-publication gate applies to every prize surveyed: Erdős (Combinatorics Foundation), Clay (refereed journal + 2 years), Beal (refereed publication of "highest editorial standards"), Bakuage (journal "generally accepted by the global Mathematical Society") — sources as cited in Q1, Q3, Q4.

### Inferences
- Base rate for a non-specialist collecting one of these prizes: effectively zero over ~50 years of Erdős prizes and 26 years of Millennium prizes. The 2026 AI wave has changed who can *produce* a candidate proof (Price) but not who *verifies, writes up and publishes* it (Tao and co-authors) or who *pays* (foundations that require journal publication).
- The realistic financial ceiling for a non-specialist in 2026 is a single Erdős prize in the $25–$500 range, contingent on (a) finding an unsolved prize problem in the ~1–2% AI-tractable tail that the frontier labs have not already swept, (b) obtaining a sorry-free Lean proof or an expert's verification, (c) getting a journal to publish it, and (d) the Combinatorics Foundation honoring it. Each step is documented; none is documented as having been completed by any non-specialist for money.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain a complete list of all Erdős prizes ever paid (Graham's records, Combinatorics Foundation records), so "zero non-specialists" is based on the sources reached, not on an exhaustive ledger.
- Backgrounds of some credited AI-era solvers (Alexeev, Sothanaphan, Bhalla, Barschkis, van Doorn, Naskrecki) were not established; some may be non-academics.
- Whether Price/Barreto were paid anything (by the Combinatorics Foundation, OpenAI, or the Justin Sun Prize) is undocumented in reachable sources.
