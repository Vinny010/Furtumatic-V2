## Key Question 1: Share of winners who are professionals/academics/teams vs solo hobbyists (Kaggle, AIMO, ARC Prize, major math prizes)

### Takeaway
The top of every arena examined is dominated by professionals, and in the highest-profile AI competitions (AIMO 2, ARC Prize 2025) the winners were literally corporate teams from NVIDIA. However, the "solo is hopeless" version of the efficient-market claim is too strong: ML Contests' cross-platform data show roughly half of competition winners competed solo (2022) and more than half of winning teams each year are first-time winners (2023), so the market is dominated by *skilled* people, not by *incumbent* people. In pure mathematics, the big-money prizes (Millennium, Breakthrough) have gone exclusively to career academics.

### Inferences
- The "efficient market" claim holds strongly for the *podium* of flagship AI competitions (AIMO 2, ARC 2025) where the winners were salaried NVIDIA researchers using 32+ H100s for training — resources that no solo amateur can match. It also holds for elite math prizes, where every large award has gone to career academics.
- It holds *weakly* for the broader competition market: ML Contests data indicate ~50% solo winners and >50% first-time winners per year, meaning the pool of winners turns over and is not a closed club. The gate is skill and time, not credentials.
- ARC Prize 2024 is the clearest evidence that individuals can podium: three of the top five were single handles, and the winners were two PhD students, not a lab. By 2025, the same competition's podium had consolidated to NVIDIA / repeat winners / a lab, suggesting that once a benchmark becomes prestigious, institutional entrants crowd in.

- ML Contests' 2024 and 2025 reports were not directly readable; solo/team share for those years is unknown.

## Key Question 2: Documented cases of outsiders/amateurs winning notable prizes or solving open problems — how common, and what did they have?

### Takeaway
Genuine outsider wins exist and are well documented, but they cluster in specific conditions: (a) newly launched or unusual-domain prizes with no incumbent expert pool (Vesuvius Challenge, ARC 2024), (b) problems accessible to computer search or constructive/counterexample approaches (einstein tile, chromatic number of the plane, Erdős #1196), and (c) "amateurs" who in fact had substantial adjacent expertise or extraordinary self-taught depth (de Grey, Cairo, van Doorn). Pure "no background + no time" wins are essentially absent from the record.

### Cited Findings

**Prize competitions**
- Vesuvius Challenge: Luke Farritor, a 21-year-old University of Nebraska–Lincoln undergraduate and SpaceX intern, won the $40,000 First Letters prize (Oct 2023) after hearing about the challenge on a podcast and "spending his evenings and late nights training a machine learning model"; his hardware was an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1070 — [Vesuvius Challenge First Letters](https://scrollprize.org/firstletters); [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-deciphers-scroll/)
- The $700,000 Vesuvius Grand Prize (Feb 2024) went to three students — Youssef Nader (biorobotics grad student, Berlin), Farritor, and Julian Schilliger (robotics student, Zurich) — [Vesuvius Challenge Grand Prize](https://scrollprize.org/grandprize); [Nebraska Today](https://news.unl.edu/article-2); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/three-students-decipher-first-passages-2000-year-old-scroll-burned-vesuvius-eruption-180983738/)
- ARC Prize 2024: winners Franzen and Disselhoff were PhD students, not a lab; three single-handle entrants took 2nd–4th — [ARC Prize on X](https://x.com/arcprize/status/1865106288012623887); [MLST podcast](https://open.spotify.com/episode/47pLT6bjNQuse4OgZ99CRA)
- Ryan Greenblatt (Redwood Research, an AI-safety nonprofit — i.e., not an ML-competition insider) reached 50% on the ARC-AGI public eval in June 2024 by sampling ~8,000 GPT-4o Python programs per task, beating the prior 34% SoTA; this was API-based and therefore ineligible for the offline Kaggle prize — [Redwood Research blog (June 17, 2024)](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/getting-50-sota-on-arc-agi-with-gpt)
- Solo ML-competition winners exist and can be efficient: ML Contests profiled Kirill Brodt, who won DrivenData's BioMassters and placed 1st or 2nd solo in five previous competitions, "mostly spending between 10 and 40 hours" on a solution — [ML Contests 2023](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-competitive-machine-learning-2023/) (via excerpt)
- Kaggle Grandmasters Chris Deotte (NVIDIA) and Fatih Öztürk have solo golds; Deotte's April 2025 solo playground win used cuML stacking — [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/grandmaster-pro-tip-winning-first-place-in-a-kaggle-competition-with-stacking-using-cuml/); [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/learning-from-others-is-imperative-to-success-on-kaggle-says-this-turkish-grandmaster-d8b5bf28ac87/) — but both are professionals, not hobbyists.

**Mathematics**
- David Smith, a retired print technician from Bridlington, England, discovered the "hat" aperiodic monotile in November 2022 using the PolyForm Puzzle Solver software and cardboard cut-outs, then contacted Craig Kaplan (Waterloo), who with Goodman-Strauss and Myers produced the proof; the problem had been open since 1966 — [Wikipedia: David Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Smith_(amateur_mathematician)); [Quanta (Apr 2023)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/hobbyist-finds-maths-elusive-einstein-tile-20230404/); [Discover](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-sciences/at-long-last-hobbyist-discovers-einstein-tile). No cash prize was attached.
- Aubrey de Grey, a professional biogerontologist and amateur mathematician, showed in April 2018 that the chromatic number of the plane is at least 5 (Hadwiger–Nelson, open since 1950) via a computer-assisted 1,581-vertex graph — [arXiv 1804.02385](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02385); [Quanta (Apr 2018)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/decades-old-graph-problem-yields-to-amateur-mathematician-20180417/); [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2018-04-amateur-mathematician-partially-year-old-problem.html). No cash prize.
- Hannah Cairo, at 17, disproved the Mizohata–Takeuchi conjecture (harmonic analysis, 1980s) with a counterexample posted to arXiv Feb 10, 2025; she was a home-schooled self-studier who emailed professors and was admitted to Ruixiang Zhang's Berkeley graduate class, where the problem was set as optional homework — [arXiv 2502.06137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06137); [Quanta (Aug 2025)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/at-17-hannah-cairo-solved-a-major-math-mystery-20250801/); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-teen-mathematician-hannah-cairo-disproved-a-major-conjecture-in-harmonic/). No cash prize.
- Wouter van Doorn, described by Quanta as "not exactly a professional mathematician" (works at a customer-service company) but also "not exactly an amateur" (nearly completed a math master's at KU Leuven a decade earlier), became one of the most prolific contributors on erdosproblems.com — [Quanta (Aug 2026)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/why-the-legendary-erdos-problems-are-falling-to-ai-20260803/)
- Liam Price, 23, "with no advanced mathematics training," typed Erdős Problem #1196 (a 1968 Erdős–Sárközy–Szemerédi conjecture on primitive sets) into GPT-5.4 Pro on April 13, 2026; the model produced a proof in ~80 minutes, which was later Lean-verified; erdosproblems.com marks it "PROVED" crediting "GPT-5.4 Pro (prompted by Liam Price)"; Terence Tao called it a "meaningful contribution to the anatomy of integers" and Tao and Lichtman worked to shorten it — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/amateur-armed-with-chatgpt-vibe-maths-a-60-year-old-problem/); [Erdős Problems #1196 thread](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/1196); [arXiv 2605.00301](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00301). No source indicated a cash prize for #1196.

### Inferences
- What the outsiders had in common: (1) a *constructive* or *computational* attack surface — tiling search, SAT/graph search, counterexample construction, program sampling, or a scanned-data ML task — rather than a need for deep theory-building; (2) an adjacent skill base (software, robotics, biology, near-master's in math) even when not credentialed in the target field; (3) large time investment ("evenings and late nights"); (4) early entry into a *new* prize before professionals arrived (Vesuvius 2023, ARC 2024).
- The Liam Price case is qualitatively different: the human contributed no mathematics; the credit line on erdosproblems.com goes to the model. This is a documented case of an amateur "winning" recognition (not cash) purely by owning a $200/month ChatGPT Pro subscription and choosing a problem, which is replicable by anyone and therefore confers no durable edge.
- Bug-bounty "solo millionaires" are real but represent roughly 30 out of a self-reported community of hundreds of thousands (HackerOne claimed 100,000+ career hackers in 2020 — [Computing.co.uk](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/4011424/hacking-bug-bounty-career)), i.e., on the order of 1 in 10,000 or rarer.

### Gaps
- No case was found of a solo amateur (no relevant professional or academic background) winning a *cash* math prize in 2024–2026. The amateur-math wins found (Smith, de Grey, Cairo, Price) were all uncompensated recognition.
- Hours invested by Smith, de Grey and Cairo are not quantified in accessible sources.

## Key Question 3: Has strong AI (2024–2026) changed who wins — leveled or concentrated? Data on LLM-assisted submissions being disqualified, flooding, or outperforming

### Takeaway
The evidence points in two directions simultaneously. In bug bounties, AI has *degraded* the arena for everyone: valid-report rates collapsed below 5% at curl, curl ended its bounty (Jan 2026), HackerOne paused the Internet Bug Bounty, and GitHub moved to an invite-only VIP tier with a four-submission cap for newcomers — raising the barrier for unknown solo entrants. In ML competitions, autonomous agents built by well-resourced labs (Meta FAIR's AIRA, OpenAI's MLE-bench work, "Disarray") now reach gold-medal level, and the AIMO 3 evidence says base-model capability swamps the clever-prompting layer where solo competitors typically add value. In mathematics, AI has produced one dramatic amateur-prompted result (Erdős #1196) but the overwhelming majority of the ~100 AI-assisted Erdős resolutions were literature searches, and credit goes to the model.

### Cited Findings

**Bug bounties: flooding and program closures**
- curl ended its bug bounty at the end of January 2026; fewer than 5% of 2025 submissions were legitimate; the confirmed-vulnerability rate fell from over 15% to under 5%; roughly one in five 2025 submissions was outright AI slop; curl's security team is seven volunteers; from Feb 1, 2026 all reports go through GitHub with no bounties — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/curl-ending-bug-bounty-program-after-flood-of-ai-slop-reports/); [Socket.dev](https://socket.dev/blog/curl-shuts-down-bug-bounty-program-after-flood-of-ai-slop-reports); [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/drowning-in-ai-slop-reports-curl-ends-bug-bounties/); [Bugcrowd opinion piece](https://www.bugcrowd.com/blog/hacker-opinion-piece-how-lazy-hacking-killed-curls-bug-bounty/)
- Industry-wide (2026): report volumes up 76% year-over-year but only ~25% flag real flaws; HackerOne paused its Internet Bug Bounty; GitHub restructured around an invite-only VIP tier and a four-submission cap for new researchers; Google and others also tightened — [DEV Community summary (2026)](https://dev.to/dmaxdev/ai-bug-bounty-in-2026-76-more-reports-programs-shutting-down-1a59); [Technology.org (May 2026)](https://www.technology.org/2026/05/18/ai-slop-bug-bounty-programs-suspended/); [AI Engineer Insights on GitHub overhaul](https://aiengineerinsights.com/blog/github-bug-bounty-ai-slop/)
- Counter-case: Joshua Rogers (an established security researcher) used the AI scanner ZeroPath plus his own filtering to send curl "a massive list" that fixed ~50 real bugs in September 2025; Daniel Stenberg called it "truly awesome findings" — [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/curl-maintainer-stenberg-says-ai-help-fix-dozens-of-bugs/); [Open Source Security podcast (Oct 2025)](https://opensourcesecurity.io/2025/2025-10-ai-joshua-rogers/); [Joshua Rogers' retrospective](https://joshua.hu/retrospective-zeropath-ai-sast-source-code-security-scanners-vulnerability)
- HackerOne 2024–25 report: total payouts $81M (+13% YoY); the top 100 earners collectively received $31.8M (≈39% of all payouts); the top 10 programs accounted for $21.6M; 61% of hackers said they will use GenAI-built tools; AI-related vulnerability reports up 200% YoY and prompt-injection reports up 540% — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackerone-paid-81-million-in-bug-bounties-over-the-past-year/); [HackerOne press release (210% AI spike)](https://www.hackerone.com/press-release/hackerone-report-finds-210-spike-ai-vulnerability-reports-amid-rise-ai-autonomy)

**ML competitions: agents and LLM-assisted winners**
- OpenAI's MLE-bench (Oct 2024) curated 75 Kaggle competitions; o1-preview with the AIDE scaffold medaled in 16.9% of them, "surpassing the average gold medal count required for a Kaggle Grandmaster (7 gold medals for o1-preview vs. 5 required)" — [arXiv 2410.07095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07095); [OpenAI MLE-bench page](https://openai.com/index/mle-bench/). - An MLE agent called "Disarray" reportedly won 28 medals across vision/NLP/tabular tasks, top-10 in nine competitions, and outperformed all human teams in one competition within 24 hours on a single GPU (April 2026 report; whether live or retrospective is unclear from the excerpt) — [AI Dynamics (Apr 9, 2026)](https://artificialintelligencedynamics.com/2026/04/09/disarray-ai-agent-wins-28-medals-in-kaggle-competitions/)

- Kaggle now hosts explicit "autonomous AI agent" competitions and ARC Prize 2026 runs ARC-AGI-2 and ARC-AGI-3 tracks — [Kaggle ARC Prize 2026 ARC-AGI-3](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3); [ARC Prize 2026](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026/arc-agi-3)

**Mathematics**
- Since October 2025, AI tools have helped resolve roughly 100 Erdős problems; of fifteen problems moving from open to solved in a recent window, eleven credited AI, but "most weren't novel insights — they were sophisticated literature searches" that located published papers — [Quanta (Aug 2026)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/why-the-legendary-erdos-problems-are-falling-to-ai-20260803/); [BuildFastWithAI summary](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/gpt-5-4-solved-a-60-year-math-problem-what-happened)
- Terence Tao's wiki of AI contributions to Erdős problems (data to June 30, 2026) categorises contributions as primary (AI standalone / alongside literature / building on literature / collaborating with humans) vs secondary (literature search, formalization, rewriting, computation); listed human contributors range from Tao, Sawhney, Alexeev, Chojecki and van Doorn to pseudonymous community members ("spiderduckpig," "vilc," "leloy"); models include GPT-5.x, Claude, Gemini, AlphaProof, Aristotle, Seed Prover, AlphaEvolve — [teorth/erdosproblems wiki](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems) (fetched directly)
- 111 Erdős problems changed from "open" to "solved" across 2024 and the first eight months of 2025 — [Quanta (Aug 2026)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/why-the-legendary-erdos-problems-are-falling-to-ai-20260803/)

### Inferences
- Bug bounties: AI has concentrated, not levelled. Programs are responding to slop by rationing access to *known* researchers (invite tiers, submission caps, program shutdowns), which is structurally hostile to a new solo entrant. The one AI success story (Rogers) came from an already-established expert who used AI as a filter-assisted scanner.
- ML competitions: the marginal contribution a solo human makes on top of a strong open model is small and shrinking (AIMO 3: ±2 points from prompting vs 8 points from model choice), so the winning move is access to the best model and the compute to run it, not cleverness. Meanwhile, autonomous agents built by labs now reach gold, and Kaggle has started to segregate them into agent-specific competitions — which suggests platforms expect agents to crowd humans out of the medal table otherwise.
- Mathematics: AI has created a new "prompt-and-pray" path (Price) that, by construction, gives every subscriber the same odds; the historically important amateur route (Smith, de Grey) required a distinctive human idea or a purpose-built search, which AI does not yet replace at scale but professionals like Tao are now systematically applying with far more sophistication.

### Gaps
- No documented case was found of a Kaggle or AIMO submission being disqualified specifically for LLM use; the flooding evidence is concentrated in bug bounties.
- Whether the "Disarray" agent's medals were live competition results or retrospective replays could not be confirmed (source page blocked).
- No data were found quantifying the share of 2025–2026 Kaggle winners who used AI coding assistants.

## Key Question 4: Time-to-payoff and opportunity cost — hours invested by winners vs prize size; expected-value estimates

### Takeaway
No credible published expected-value model for a solo competitor was found. What exists is scattered: elite ML competitors report 3–4-day sprints at 5–12 hours/day or 5–10 hours/week over three months; one efficient solo winner reports 10–40 hours per winning solution; bug-bounty medians are low ($2,000 on Immunefi; most HackerOne hackers under $20k/year) with extreme right tails. Practitioner consensus, including Kaggle's own community, is that competitions should be treated as portfolio/skill investment, "not as a lottery ticket for prize money."

### Cited Findings
- Top Kagglers work in 3–4-day sprints of 5–12 hours/day; one Grandmaster reported 3 months at 5–10 hours/week on a single competition — [Quora thread](https://www.quora.com/How-much-time-does-one-need-to-invest-into-kaggle-competitions); [Kaggle discussion](https://www.kaggle.com/general/395168)
- Solo serial winner Kirill Brodt "mostly spent between 10 and 40 hours" per winning/second-place solution — [ML Contests 2023](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-competitive-machine-learning-2023/) (via excerpt)
- Typical Kaggle competition: ~3 months, $25k–$100k total prize purse, ~1,000 competitors — [Danil Zherebtsov, Medium](https://danilzherebtsov.medium.com/new-kind-of-kaggle-competitions-0605e88a66e0)
- Total ML competition prize pool: $7.8m+ across 300+ competitions in 2023; $16m+ across 390+ competitions on 30+ platforms in 2025 — [ML Contests 2023](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-competitive-machine-learning-2023/); [ML Contests 2025](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2025/) (via excerpts)
- "Kaggle is worth it, but only when you treat it as a public workshop for skill proof, not as a lottery ticket for prize money" — [Refonte Learning (2026)](https://www.refontelearning.com/blog/best-kaggle-competitions-for-beginners-2026)
- HackerOne 2020 Hacker Report: 18% of hackers describe themselves as full-time; about 12% make $20k+/year from bounties, over 3% make $100k+/year, and 1% make $350k+/year; a quarter rely on bounties for at least half of their income — [HackerOne 2020 press release](https://www.hackerone.com/press-release/hacking-career-soars-popularity-according-hackerones-2020-hacker-report); [Security Magazine](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/91785-hackerones-2020-hacker-report-hacking-as-a-career-soars-in-popularity); [Computing.co.uk](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/4011424/hacking-bug-bounty-career)
- Immunefi: median confirmed payout ~$2,000 vs average ~$52,800; for *critical* bugs median $20,000 vs mean $114,355 (skewed by a $10M payout); 45,000+ researchers registered; $134M cumulative paid by end of Q1 2026 with $7.87M in Q1 across 1,104 reports — [SQ Magazine (2026)](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/smart-contract-bug-bounties-statistics/); [Immunefi research blog](https://immunefi.com/blog/research/nearly-every-long-running-bug-bounty-program-on-immunefi-has-found-a-critical-bug/); [Immunefi on X](https://x.com/immunefi/status/2062543462939963867)
- Vesuvius Challenge: Farritor's $40k First Letters prize came from evenings/late nights over roughly the summer–autumn of 2023 on a GTX 1070 — [Vesuvius Challenge](https://scrollprize.org/firstletters); [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-deciphers-scroll/)
- Erdős prizes: $25 to $10,000; the largest ($10,000) was split among five professional mathematicians — [Quanta, Cash for Math (2017)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/cash-for-math-the-erdos-prizes-live-on-20170605/)

### Inferences
- Back-of-envelope from the cited numbers: a $50k Kaggle purse spread over ~1,000 entrants is ~$50 of prize per entrant on average, and gold goes to ~10–12 teams; a competent solo entrant spending 100–200 hours has a realistic ceiling of a bronze/silver (no cash) unless they are already near Grandmaster level. This is an inference, not a published EV estimate.
- Immunefi's $134M cumulative over 45,000 registered researchers is roughly $3,000 per registered researcher lifetime, with a median payout of $2,000 per confirmed report and most registrants never paid — consistent with a "long tail of occasional participants and a small core who treat it as a full-time job" ([HOGE Wire](https://hoge.gg/crypto-bug-bounty-hunters-whitehat-workforce/)).
- The only documented "fast" wins (Brodt's 10–40 hours) came from a serial winner with a pre-built toolkit; first wins consistently required hundreds of hours.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or credible blog EV model for a solo Kaggle/AIMO competitor was found. An OUP *Journal of Cybersecurity* paper builds an economic model of bug-bounty markets from HackerOne data but could not be accessed here — [Journal of Cybersecurity, tyab007](https://academic.oup.com/cybersecurity/article-pdf/doi/10.1093/cybsec/tyab007/36578302/tyab007.pdf).
- HackerOne's 2020 percentages are self-reported survey data, six years old; no comparable 2025–2026 income-distribution breakdown was found.

## Key Question 5: "Compute edge" — do one or two consumer GPUs confer any advantage in 2026, or have hosted/free compute and API models neutralised it?

### Takeaway
A pair of consumer GPUs is neither an edge nor a hard blocker; it is table stakes. Winners of flagship competitions train on 32+ H100s (NVARC, WSDM Cup 2025) while inference is capped by the organiser (AIMO 3: one H100, 5 hours), so the leaderboard-relevant compute is either far beyond a consumer rig (training) or provided for free (inference). Consumer cards remain common among winners of mid-tier competitions (RTX 4090/3090 were the most popular consumer cards in 2024; RTX 5090 appeared in 2025 winning solutions), and one famous outsider win (Vesuvius) was done on a GTX 1070 — but the 2026 AIMO evidence says the decisive variable is which open model you can run, not how much local compute you own.

### Cited Findings
- ML Contests 2025: H100 dethroned A100 as the most-used GPU among winning solutions; RTX 5090 and RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell appeared in winning solutions; all winning solutions used NVIDIA GPUs or CPU only; only six winners were identifiable as using personal hardware and three as using rented cloud, with most unidentifiable — [ML Contests 2025](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2025/) (via excerpt)
- ML Contests 2024: RTX 4090 and RTX 3090 were the most popular consumer-grade cards among winners — [ML Contests 2024](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2024/) (via excerpt)
- ML Contests 2023: over 70% of winners used a GPU, "many of these used powerful data-centre GPUs rather than cheaper consumer-oriented ones"; some winners trained on servers with 8x RTX A6000 or a DGX-1 with 8x V100 — [ML Contests 2023](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-competitive-machine-learning-2023/) (via excerpt)
- ARC Prize 2025 winner NVARC and the WSDM Cup 2025 winner each trained on four 8xH100 nodes (32 H100s) — [ML Contests 2025](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2025/) (via excerpt); the ARC Prize organisers themselves adapted a solution on 4 nodes of 8 H100s — [ARC Prize 2025 details](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2025)
- ARC Prize's stated efficiency metric: the winning 4B model runs at $0.20/task — [ARC Prize 2025 Results](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis)
- AIMO 2 inference budget: 4x L4 GPUs, 5 hours; AIMO 3: one H100 80–85 GB, 5 hours, open-weight models only (released before Mar 15, 2026); the H100 offers "roughly double the compute power of AIMO2," enabling GPT-OSS-120B and Qwen3-Next — [NVIDIA on X](https://x.com/NVIDIAAIDev/status/1910383538027061764); [arXiv 2603.27844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27844); [Kaggle AIMO3 writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ai-mathematical-olympiad-progress-prize-3/writeups/entropy-weighted-majority-voting-with-gpt-oss-120b)
- A non-affiliated AIMO 3 competitor reports developing a 41.5/50 solution entirely on Kaggle-provided compute with no personal GPU or cloud spend mentioned — [Jarvis2001/AIMO3-Writeup](https://github.com/Jarvis2001/AIMO3-Writeup)
- AIMO 3 evidence that model capability, not local optimisation, dominates outcomes (8.3-point model gap vs ±2-point prompting effects) — [arXiv 2603.27844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27844)
- Vesuvius First Letters ($40k) was won on a single GeForce GTX 1070 — [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-deciphers-scroll/)
- Greenblatt's 50% ARC result used ~8,000 GPT-4o samples per task via API, which made it ineligible for the offline Kaggle prize — [Redwood Research](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/getting-50-sota-on-arc-agi-with-gpt)
- Disarray agent reportedly outperformed all human teams in one competition within 24 hours on a single GPU — [AI Dynamics (Apr 2026)](https://artificialintelligencedynamics.com/2026/04/09/disarray-ai-agent-wins-28-medals-in-kaggle-competitions/)
- Kaggle standard notebooks provide 2x T4 or 2x P100; AIMO 3 provided enhanced H100 access — [Jarvis2001/AIMO3-Writeup](https://github.com/Jarvis2001/AIMO3-Writeup)

### Inferences
- Where the organiser fixes an inference budget (AIMO, ARC), a home rig is useful only for *offline* experimentation and fine-tuning; the 1st-place AIMO 3 solution's key ideas (model choice, early stopping on consensus) are testable on Kaggle's free H100 hours, which is how at least one unaffiliated competitor iterated 56 versions.
- Where training compute is unconstrained (ARC training phase, most Kaggle vision/NLP competitions), the top of the leaderboard is set by 8–32 datacentre GPUs; two consumer cards (roughly 48 GB total VRAM) cannot replicate synthetic-data pipelines of "hundreds of thousands of samples" through a 120B model in a competition window.
- The one arena where consumer GPUs were decisive (Vesuvius 2023) was a *new, data-limited* problem with small models; that window closed once well-funded teams arrived.
- Net: in 2026, a solo entrant's compute is not the binding constraint; the binding constraints are (a) hours of expert iteration, (b) access to the strongest permitted open model, and (c) whether the arena still has undiscovered low-hanging fruit.

### Gaps
- ML Contests' owned-vs-rented hardware data are sparse by their own admission (six owned, three rented, rest unknown), so no percentage claim about "winners with only consumer GPUs" can be made.
- No source quantifies how many hours of free Kaggle/Colab compute AIMO 3 entrants actually consumed, or whether any 2025–2026 winner trained exclusively on a home consumer rig.
