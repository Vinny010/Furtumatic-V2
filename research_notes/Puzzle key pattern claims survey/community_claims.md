## Key Question 1 — What did the creator actually say about key generation, and what does it imply for predictability?

### Takeaway
The only creator statement anyone cites is a single 2017-04-27 bitcointalk post: "There is no pattern. It is just consecutive keys from a deterministic wallet (masked with leading 000...0001 to set difficulty). It is simply a crude measuring instrument, of the cracking strength of the community." Every serious attempt to exploit "deterministic wallet" (recover the seed/PRNG from the 69–83 known keys) has failed, and the community's own analyses conclude that a BIP32-style chain code makes child keys non-recoverable from siblings.

### Cited Findings
- Verbatim creator quote, attributed to saatoshi_rising, bitcointalk topic 1306983, dated 2017-04-27 06:41:08, message id msg18765941, with a Wayback capture cited as confirmation — [agntn/puzzles landing.ts (collection hint record)](https://github.com/agntn/puzzles/blob/main/docs/app/utils/landing.ts); same quote and date in [itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list](https://github.com/itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list/blob/main/solved/bitcoin-puzzle-transaction-solved.md); quote (undated) also in [roadhero/Bitcoin-Puzzle-Info README](https://github.com/roadhero/Bitcoin-Puzzle-Info) and [HomelessPhD/BTC32 README](https://github.com/HomelessPhD/BTC32). (The bitcointalk post itself could not be fetched — blocked.)
- Community reading of the mask: "Private Key = 2^(n-1) + (wallet_key_n & (2^(n-1) - 1))", i.e. the top bit is forced to 1 and the lower n-1 bits are taken from the n-th wallet key — [mlartab/bitcoin-puzzle-systematic-analysis README](https://github.com/mlartab/bitcoin-puzzle-systematic-analysis).
- The creator's handle "remains anonymous in any provable sense, though the handle most commonly cited … is saatoshi_rising", per a 2026 news summary (snippet only) — [news.bitcoin.com](https://news.bitcoin.com/key-hunters-eye-58-87m-bitcoin-puzzle-as-916-btc-sits-unsolved-in-78-addresses/).
- Creator actions that changed the game: 2017-07-11 moved funds from #161–#256 into #1–#160 (10x prize); 2019-05-31 / 2019-06-01 sent 1,000-sat outputs from every 5th address (#65, #70, #75 … #160), exposing their public keys and enabling Pollard kangaroo; 2023-04-16 topped up prizes (e.g. +12.15 BTC to #135) — [roadhero README](https://github.com/roadhero/Bitcoin-Puzzle-Info); tx-level list for #135 in [agntn/puzzles](https://github.com/agntn/puzzles/blob/main/docs/app/utils/landing.ts).
- **Seed/PRNG recovery attempts (the "deterministic wallet ⇒ predictable" hypothesis, tested):** mlartab enumerated all 2^32 seeds of Python's Mersenne Twister ("No seed worked. The puzzle wasn't made with Python's random"), all 2^32 glibc rand() seeds ("Nothing"), Java's LCG via algebraic state recovery ("The puzzle keys don't match Java's LCG pattern"), MT19937 state recovery with Z3 and the `untwister` tool ("The SAT solver couldn't crack it"), millions of BIP32 passphrases across four derivation paths (rockyou.txt, bitcoin vocabulary, creator-associated phrases: "The passphrase is not in any public wordlist"), and pre-BIP32 SHA256 brainwallets with several separators ("Nothing"). Author: "I didn't find the key to puzzle #71." — [mlartab README](https://github.com/mlartab/bitcoin-puzzle-systematic-analysis).
- Same author's structural conclusion: with BIP32, "Because SHA-512 is a one-way function, you cannot … compute child keys without the chain code" and "The chain code is 256 random bits. Brute-forcing it would take longer than the age of the universe." — [mlartab README](https://github.com/mlartab/bitcoin-puzzle-systematic-analysis).
- lutfiEmre's "puzzle-71-research" checked whether the addresses came from compressed or uncompressed pubkeys (82/82 solved outputs are compressed; the 2017 redistribution tx had 96 compressed + 1 uncompressed inputs) and tested the Casascius "SHA256(i/passphrase/i/BITCOIN)" formula; it explicitly states this "Does not prove BIP32 or any named wallet", that "with current public evidence there is no verifiable, computationally justified solution path", and closed the project `CLOSED_PENDING_NEW_PRIMARY_EVIDENCE` — [PUBKEY_FINGERPRINT.md](https://github.com/lutfiEmre/bitcoin-puzzle-71-research/blob/main/PUBKEY_FINGERPRINT.md); [README](https://github.com/lutfiEmre/bitcoin-puzzle-71-research).
- graffitilogic generated "a billion keys using from-the-era hardware and OSs" looking for OpenSSL `bn_rand_range` grouping anomalies; conclusion "I don't think there is anything here" and "I'm not claiming any breakthroughs here!" — [graffitilogic/KeyScanner](https://github.com/graffitilogic/KeyScanner).
- Consecutive-key relationship check (own computation from the HomelessPhD file): ratio k(n+1)/k(n) for n=5..69 ranges 1.10–3.37, mean 2.07 — exactly what independent uniform draws in doubling ranges give; no usable recurrence. Lag-1 autocorrelation of the normalised positions for #5–#70 is 0.066 (noise level). — computed by this researcher from [BTC32_solved_unsolved.txt](https://github.com/HomelessPhD/BTC32/blob/main/BTC32_solved_unsolved.txt).

### Inferences
- "Deterministic wallet" is not exploitable in practice: every attempt to reproduce the key stream from cheap generators or wordlists failed, and if the wallet is BIP32-like the sibling keys give no information about each other. The statement is best read as "I did not hand-pick the numbers", not as a hint.
- The mask explains the only structural "pattern" anyone can observe (the forced top bit / leading hex digit distribution, see KQ2); nothing below the top bit has yielded to analysis.

### Gaps
- The original 2017 post and any follow-up creator posts (e.g. the 2019 pubkey-reveal announcement, any 2023 comment) could not be read directly; the quote is reproduced from secondary repos that cite msg18765941 and a Wayback capture.
- No source names the wallet software used; the Casascius-formula and BIP32 hypotheses are untested guesses.

## Key Question 2 — Position-in-range / "hot zone" theories, and how they fared against the solved keys

### Takeaway
Three position claims circulate: (a) HomelessPhD/BTC32's 2021–2023 histogram observation that keys cluster at 30–50 % and 60–80 % of their range with a "special" 82–83 % cluster; (b) tracker statistics ("average 53.5 %, 41 of 79 above 50 %") that some readers turn into "keys are usually in the upper half / 60–70 % band"; (c) a general "tendency toward the centre (0.5)". Against all 83 keys the positions are statistically indistinguishable from uniform (decile chi-square 8.7 on 9 df, KS D=0.075, p≈0.76), the author of (a) himself warned the peaks were probably small-sample noise, and #69 (0.72 %) and #25/#60/#63 (>95 %) fall in the "dead" zones the theories would skip.

### Cited Findings
- HomelessPhD/BTC32 (the most-cited statistical write-up, by an anonymous GitHub user, first published 2021, using ~69 keys #1–#63, #65, #70, #75…#135): defines alpha = (PK − 2^(n−1))/2^(n−1) ∈ [0,1); histograms suggested "Private Keys are more probable to be found at {0.3-0.5} and {0.6-0.8}" with "special case … {0.82-0.83} where few Private Keys lied closely". He offered three competing scenarios — (1) peaks are predictive, (2) future keys will fill the gaps, (3) "Each Private Key is generated using a strong (good) Random Generator and so all of them are uniformly distributed - the histogram has its peaks due to small 'observations number'". His tests: "there is no any linear tendency or relation in data that is shown by its correlation. Autocorrelation looks just like a noise autocorrelation"; a MATLAB simulation of the same generation scheme showed "the histogram for the first 60s numbers doesn't linked with the rest 100s histogram", i.e. scenarios 1 and 2 "could be a mistake"; and he warned that an order-n polynomial "will ideally fit the dataset of n points … just memorizing the data instead of retrieving the logic behind the data." — [HomelessPhD/BTC32 README](https://github.com/HomelessPhD/BTC32).
- Tracker statistic (snippet only, attributed by the search engine to secretscan.org): "79 keys solved, average found at 53.5 %, 41 keys (51.9 %) found above the 50 % mark" — [secretscan.org/Bitcoin_puzzle](https://secretscan.org/Bitcoin_puzzle) (blocked; wording from search snippet).
- A "tendency of acquiring values closer to the center (0.5)" is mentioned in a search summary of the HomelessPhD analysis (snippet only) — [HomelessPhD/BTC32](https://github.com/HomelessPhD/BTC32).
- Trackers describe #69's 0.72 % position as the reason it fell quickly: "Puzzle #69 (6.9 BTC) was solved by bc1qlp in less than one month, likely due to its position at the very beginning of the search range (0.72 %)" (snippet only) — [privatekeys.pw](https://privatekeys.pw/puzzles/bitcoin-puzzle-tx); also [roadhero README](https://github.com/roadhero/Bitcoin-Puzzle-Info); [itsnex1s](https://github.com/itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list/blob/main/solved/bitcoin-puzzle-transaction-solved.md) ("0.72 % into its interval—fell within one month of #68").
- **Independent recomputation by this researcher (all 83 keys, from the HomelessPhD file, alpha = (k−2^(n−1))/2^(n−1)):**
  - #66 25.62 %, #67 79.78 %, #68 49.01 %, #69 0.72 %, #70 64.40 %, #75 19.32 %, #80 82.89 %, #85 9.03 %, #90 40.23 %, #95 28.87 %, #100 36.98 %, #105 43.39 %, #110 67.98 %, #115 51.49 %, #120 38.33 %, #125 77.03 %, #130 62.20 %, #135 71.21 %.
  - For n ≥ 5 (79 keys): mean 0.514, median 0.502, 50.6 % above 0.5. Decile counts [5,7,7,10,10,5,14,8,6,7] vs 7.9 expected → chi-square 8.72 (9 df, p≈0.46). Kolmogorov–Smirnov D = 0.075, p ≈ 0.76. Lag-1 autocorrelation 0.066.
  - "Hot zones": 14 keys in 60–70 %, 20 in 30–50 %, 22 in 60–80 %, 5 in 82–83 % (0.82–0.83 is a 1 %-wide bin; expectation ≈0.8, so this is the one visually striking cluster — #9 82.42 %, #40 82.56 %, #51 82.86 %, #59 82.17 %, #80 82.89 % — but it is a post-hoc bin among ~100 such bins).
  - Extremes that any "skip the edges" rule would miss: below 5 %: #10 (0.39 %), #69 (0.72 %); above 95 %: #25 (97.8 %), #31 (95.8 %), #60 (96.9 %), #63 (95.0 %).
  - Note on the "#70 at 40 %" figure in the task brief: #70's key 0x349b84b6431a6c4ef1 sits at 64.4 % of [2^69, 2^70) (or 82.2 % if measured as k/2^70). The nearest key to 40 % is #90 (40.23 %). Some sites measure position over the full [0, 2^n) span, which shifts every value upward (#66 → 62.8 %, #69 → 50.4 %); readers should check which convention a claim uses.
  — computed from [BTC32_solved_unsolved.txt](https://github.com/HomelessPhD/BTC32/blob/main/BTC32_solved_unsolved.txt).
- Leading-hex-digit "theory" check: first hex digit counts for n ≥ 5 are {1:20, 2:11, 3:9, 4:5, 5:3, 6:7, 7:5, 9:2, a:3, b:3, c:1, d:3, e:5, f:2}; the excess of 1/2/3 is entirely the forced top bit (when (n−1) mod 4 = 0 the leading digit must be 1; when = 1 it must be 2 or 3, etc.), not a property of the wallet. — own computation from the same file.
- Free-bit balance: fraction of 1-bits among the n−1 free bits (n ≥ 20) ranges 0.348–0.667, mean 0.495 — consistent with fair coin flips. — own computation from the same file.
- Time-ordered evidence against "hot zones": #66 (25.6 %) and #69 (0.72 %) both landed outside the HomelessPhD 30–50/60–80 bands published years earlier; #67 (79.8 %) and #68 (49.0 %) landed inside them. 2 of 4 post-publication brute-force solves are hits, i.e. chance level for bands covering ~40 % of the range.

### Inferences
- The solved positions behave like i.i.d. uniform draws; any "band" theory built on ≤83 points has no statistical support, and the one author who published a histogram explicitly declined to endorse it.
- The 53.5 % / 51.9 %-above-half tracker numbers are within one standard error of 50 % (sd of the mean of 79 uniforms ≈ 0.033) and cannot support an "upper-half" strategy.

### Gaps
- Could not read the bitcointalk main-thread posts where "60–70 % band"-type claims were made or rebutted (blocked); no snippet surfaced an explicit "60–70 % band" post, so the exact originator of that phrasing is unidentified.
- The secretscan.org statistic page and privatekeys.pw commentary were visible only as snippets.

## Key Question 3 — Tools/repos claiming a non-uniform scan order improves odds, and what their results show

### Takeaway
Several public tools filter or re-order the keyspace by bit balance, repeated-character "low entropy", pattern frequency, address-prefix matching, "analytics-guided" bit patterns, or 0/1-count constraints (e.g. "reduce the key space to 44.56 % of puzzle 66"). None documents a solve, a benchmark, or a derivation from the solved keys; the two public statistics on solved keys (bit balance 0.35–0.67 and uniform positions) show these filters can only discard valid regions with no compensating gain. All documented solves were plain exhaustive/random brute force (≤ #69) or kangaroo (exposed-pubkey lots).

### Cited Findings
- **OlivierGaland/bitcoin-puzzle-worker** (client for a pool): "try to select only range that are statistically more likely to be the solution of the puzzle with the hope of finding the solution faster." Filters: FILTER_BASE_DISPLAY ("discard range repeating to many times same char like for instance 0xAAAAA789 in base16"), FILTER_BIT_BALANCE ("0.2 means that bits with same value must be from 30 % … to 70 % of total bits"), FILTER_PATTERN (e.g. `FILTER_PATTERN_000=010,0.03,0.15`). "No empirical results are provided"; filters optional; software "under developpement". — [README](https://github.com/OlivierGaland/bitcoin-puzzle-worker).
- **allinbit/btc_puzzle**: "The number of 0 and 1 in a 66-bit binary number can be specified to reduce the key space. It is calculated that if combinations of 0 or 1 with a probability of occurrence greater than 41 % are used, the key space will be reduced to about 44.56 % of the puzzle 66." No derivation, no results, users told to "try your luck". — [README](https://github.com/allinbit/btc_puzzle). (Outcome: #66 was solved 2024-09-12 by plain brute force; its key 0x2832ed74f2b5e35ee has a free-bit 1-fraction that this author did not check against the 41 % rule — see gap.)
- **binqbit/bit-scan**: "v2 uses the same cryptographic RNG as an input to its intentionally non-uniform, analytics-guided bit-pattern generator." No description of the patterns, no results; README just lists the 70 solved keys. — [README](https://github.com/binqbit/bit-scan).
- **ACD421/bitcoin-puzzle-solvers**: advertises "Pollard Rho, SAT encoding, kangaroo, GA, neural approaches"; the "Neural Hybrid" is "TensorFlow model trained to predict high-fitness regions for guided search" (`puzzle135_hybrid_ai.py`); "no reported results, performance metrics, or success claims". — [README](https://github.com/ACD421/bitcoin-puzzle-solvers). (#135 was subsequently solved by RetiredCoder with kangaroo, not by this.)
- **accessor-io/pattern**: "Focused Search — Explores values around specific predicted ranges", "Mathematical Patterns", "Bitwise Operations", a "RowHammer-Inspired" bit-flip approach targeting #68 from #67's key; "the search has generated 321,270 unique candidates but has not yet found a match". — [repo](https://github.com/accessor-io/pattern). (#68 was later solved by brute force at 49.0 %.)
- **sjgant80-hub/cassietorusbtc135solver**: kangaroo with a "torus topology" (x mod 510510 folds, "phi-irrational spacing"), targeting #135; "no actual solved puzzles or empirical performance data". — [repo](https://github.com/sjgant80-hub/cassietorusbtc135solver).
- **Address-prefix approaches** ("Bitcoin Puzzle 71 Prefixes" YouTube channel; VanitySearch-style): publish keys in the #71 range whose address matches the first 8 characters of the target (snippet only) — [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@BitcoinPuzzle71Prefixes). A bitcointalk thread "BITCOIN PUZZLE: THE PREFIX DILEMMA" was opened 2025-11-07 (snippet only; content unreadable) — [topic 5564718](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=5564718.0). Prefix matching is a near-miss detector, not a keyspace prior; nothing found claims it locates the key.
- **AI-prompt products**: a docsbot.ai prompt "Predict BTC Puzzle Range" purports "to determine the numeric range for Bitcoin puzzle number 71 based on previous puzzle data patterns" (snippet only; page blocked) — [docsbot.ai](https://docsbot.ai/prompts/technical/predict-btc-puzzle-range).
- **Pools' own numbers:** btcpuzzle.info #71 pool (sequential/assigned hex sub-ranges, no prior) as of 2026-06-23 had scanned 290,012 of 33,554,432 ranges (0.864 %) at 57.3 Gkeys/s, projected 421.92 years (snippet only) — [btcpuzzle.info/puzzle/71](https://btcpuzzle.info/puzzle/71) via [news.bitcoin.com](https://news.bitcoin.com/key-hunters-eye-58-87m-bitcoin-puzzle-as-916-btc-sits-unsolved-in-78-addresses/). The ilkerccom "bitcrackrandomiser" pool for #66–#68 handed out random ranges — [repo](https://github.com/ilkerccom/bitcrackrandomiser).
- **What actually solved things:** Large Bitcoin Collider swept sequentially "from the bottom"; its log shows 51-bit completion 2017-04-09 and 52-bit 2017-04-23, matching #51/#52 solve dates — [itsnex1s](https://github.com/itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list/blob/main/solved/bitcoin-puzzle-transaction-solved.md). JeanLucPons/Kangaroo "solved puzzle #110 in 2.1 days (109 bit key) using 256 Tesla V100 … 2^55.55 group operations using DP25" — same source. #135: RetiredCoder, kangaroo DP=32, ~200 GPUs (RTX 4090/5090), ~5 months, "finishing at roughly one-third of expected theoretical work"; quote "Yeah, finally I solved #135, it took about 5 months on 200GPUs … I quit, officially." (bitcointalk topic 5589799, 2026-07-29); RCKangaroo v4.0 released 95 minutes later — [itsnex1s](https://github.com/itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list/blob/main/solved/bitcoin-puzzle-transaction-solved.md); solver/date/tx also in [agntn/puzzles](https://github.com/agntn/puzzles/blob/main/docs/app/utils/landing.ts).
- Solve methods #66–#70 per trackers: #66 2024-09-12 brute force, key leaked in mempool and front-run (406 sat/vB); #67 2025-02-21 and #68 2025-04-06 brute force (address-only), submitted out-of-band (MARA Slipstream per roadhero) to avoid front-running; #69 2025-04-30 brute force, 0.72 %, mempool fee war won at 2,515 sat/vB; #70 2019-06-09 kangaroo on the pubkey exposed 2019-06-01 — [roadhero README](https://github.com/roadhero/Bitcoin-Puzzle-Info); [itsnex1s](https://github.com/itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list/blob/main/solved/bitcoin-puzzle-transaction-solved.md); solver handles "Kowala" for #67/#68, "bc1qlp" #69, "pikachunakapika" #70/#75/#80/#85 — [SamuelOliveiraBRA/btc-hunter-julia README](https://github.com/SamuelOliveiraBRA/btc-hunter-julia). **Conflict:** itsnex1s labels #67/#68 "Kangaroo on exposed public key", but no pubkey for #67/#68 was exposed before the solver's own spend; roadhero and the news summaries describe them as GPU brute force. Treat itsnex1s's method label for #67/#68 as an error.
- "Puzzles up to 70 bits fell to raw GPU brute force: tools like BitCrack and KeyHunt walking the keyspace" (snippet only) — [btcpuzzlesearch.com/solved](https://btcpuzzlesearch.com/solved).

### Inferences
- No non-uniform scan order has a documented success; every tool that advertises one either reports no result or an explicit failure. Because free-bit balance across the 83 keys spans 0.35–0.67 and positions are uniform, entropy/pattern filters remove real probability mass (e.g. #69's key 0x101d83275fb2bc7e0c is exactly the kind of "low-entropy, near-boundary" value such filters discard).
- The statement "an optimal solver should evaluate candidates in order of strictly decreasing likelihood, which requires a structural property in the key distribution" (search summary of an unidentified page) is correct but vacuous here: with a flat distribution every order has equal expected cost.

### Gaps
- Could not read bitcointalk discussions of KeyHunt/BitCrack "prefix skipping" or the "Prefix Dilemma" thread; who proposed it and the rebuttals are unknown beyond the title/date.
- No repo publishes a before/after benchmark of a filter on already-solved puzzles; the claim "44.56 % of puzzle 66" was never checked against #66's actual key in any source found.

## Key Question 4 — Rigorous statistical write-ups (chi-square, KS) of solved-key positions

### Takeaway
Only one community write-up (HomelessPhD/BTC32) does formal statistics (histograms, correlation, autocorrelation, simulation), and it ends without claiming a pattern. No chi-square or KS test on the solved positions was found in any public source; this researcher's own tests on the 83 keys give chi-square 8.72 (9 df) and KS p≈0.76 — no evidence against uniformity.

### Cited Findings
- HomelessPhD/BTC32: alpha histograms with several bin widths; "no any linear tendency or relation … Autocorrelation looks just like a noise autocorrelation"; MATLAB simulation showing early-sample histograms do not predict later ones; overfitting warning. No p-values reported. — [README](https://github.com/HomelessPhD/BTC32).
- graffitilogic/KeyScanner: empirical "grouping anomaly" analysis of 10^9 OpenSSL-era keys; "I don't think there is anything here." — [repo](https://github.com/graffitilogic/KeyScanner).
- mlartab: no distribution statistics, only exhaustive PRNG/seed elimination (all negative) — [README](https://github.com/mlartab/bitcoin-puzzle-systematic-analysis).
- Web search for "kolmogorov"/"chi-square"/"p-value" together with the puzzle returned only generic randomness-testing papers; no puzzle-specific test write-up exists in indexed sources — [search context: Diehard tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diehard_tests).
- This researcher's computation (n ≥ 5, 79 keys): mean 0.514, median 0.502, decile counts [5,7,7,10,10,5,14,8,6,7], chi-square 8.72 (p≈0.46), KS D=0.0754 (p≈0.76), lag-1 autocorrelation 0.066, free-bit 1-fraction 0.35–0.67 (mean 0.495) — from [BTC32_solved_unsolved.txt](https://github.com/HomelessPhD/BTC32/blob/main/BTC32_solved_unsolved.txt). (Method: alpha=(k−2^(n−1))/2^(n−1); asymptotic KS p-value; puzzles #1–#4 excluded because ranges of ≤8 values make alpha meaningless.)

### Inferences
- With 83 samples the tests have little power against subtle biases, but they rule out the strong claims (bands of 20–40 % width holding most keys, or edges being empty) that would be needed to speed a search materially.

### Gaps
- Any bitcointalk post applying formal tests (users such as kTimesG, WanderingPhilospher, nomachine are reputed sceptics in snippets, but their posts were unreadable) — none confirmed.
- Reddit content unreachable; no Reddit statistical write-up identified.

## Key Question 5 — Documented solves via a claimed pattern vs. brute force / kangaroo

### Takeaway
Zero solves are attributed to a pattern, heuristic filter, or AI prior. All 83 public keys came from exhaustive/random-range brute force (#1–#69 except #65/#70, incl. LBC sweeps and GPU pools) or Pollard kangaroo on creator-exposed public keys (#65, #70, #75–#135 step 5); the few "pattern" repos that name a target (#68, #135) were beaten by conventional methods.

### Cited Findings
- Solve table (method/date): #66 brute force 2024-09-12 (front-run); #67 2025-02-21 and #68 2025-04-06 brute force, private mempool submission; #69 brute force 2025-04-30 at 0.72 %; #70 kangaroo 2019-06-09; #75–#85 kangaroo June 2019 (pikachunakapika); #110/#115 JeanLucPons Kangaroo 2020; #120 2023-02-27; #125 2023-07-09; #130 2024-09-23; #135 RetiredCoder kangaroo 2026-07-28 — [itsnex1s](https://github.com/itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list/blob/main/solved/bitcoin-puzzle-transaction-solved.md); [btc-hunter-julia](https://github.com/SamuelOliveiraBRA/btc-hunter-julia); [HomelessPhD file](https://github.com/HomelessPhD/BTC32/blob/main/BTC32_solved_unsolved.txt); [roadhero](https://github.com/roadhero/Bitcoin-Puzzle-Info).
- "One stark pattern — no address-only lot above #70 has fallen in 11 years. All high-numbered solves (except #66, #69) required exposed public keys" — [itsnex1s](https://github.com/itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list/blob/main/solved/bitcoin-puzzle-transaction-solved.md).
- #135 finished "at roughly one-third of expected theoretical work" — luck within kangaroo's variance, not a prior — [itsnex1s](https://github.com/itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list/blob/main/solved/bitcoin-puzzle-transaction-solved.md).
- accessor-io/pattern (targeting #68 via transformations of #67's key) reported no match; #68 later fell to brute force — [repo](https://github.com/accessor-io/pattern).
- ACD421 neural/GA and cassietorus solvers targeting #135 reported no results; #135 fell to RCKangaroo — [ACD421](https://github.com/ACD421/bitcoin-puzzle-solvers); [cassietorus](https://github.com/sjgant80-hub/cassietorusbtc135solver).
- Historical mechanism of early solves: LBC sequential sweep timings match #51/#52 dates — [itsnex1s](https://github.com/itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list/blob/main/solved/bitcoin-puzzle-transaction-solved.md).
- Post-#66 "solves" were in part thefts: the #66 key was extracted from the mempool and the prize front-run; #69's winner paid 2,515 sat/vB in a replacement auction — [roadhero](https://github.com/roadhero/Bitcoin-Puzzle-Info); [itsnex1s](https://github.com/itsnex1s/crypto-puzzle-list/blob/main/solved/bitcoin-puzzle-transaction-solved.md); Bitcoin Core issue on RBF/bots for these puzzles — [bitcoin/bitcoin#29793](https://github.com/bitcoin/bitcoin/issues/29793).

### Inferences
- The only "pattern" that demonstrably shortened a solve is the creator's own choice of which pubkeys to expose (halving the exponent), plus luck in where individual keys happened to sit (#69 at 0.72 %, #10 at 0.39 %).

### Gaps
- Solver-identity claims (Kowala, bc1qlp, pikachunakapika) are tracker/forum attributions from repos, not verified statements by the solvers; bitcointalk posts were unreadable.
- Whether the #67/#68 solvers (reportedly the same party) used any range prior is undocumented; both keys lie at 79.8 % and 49.0 %, giving no hint of an edge-first or centre-first order.
