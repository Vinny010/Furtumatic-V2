## Key Question 1: For each non-generic approach — what does it require, what is its complexity, and does secp256k1 satisfy the conditions?

### Takeaway
Every known *structural* (non-generic, sub-exponential or polynomial) ECDLP attack requires a curve property that secp256k1 was explicitly chosen not to have: small embedding degree (MOV/Frey–Rück), trace 1 (Smart/Satoh–Araki/Semaev anomalous attack), or an extension-field definition (GHS/Weil descent and all practical summation-polynomial index-calculus variants). The attacks that *do* touch secp256k1 are either constant-factor speed-ups of Pollard rho (negation map + order-6 automorphism, total √6), implementation-level attacks that need an unvalidated-input bug (twist/invalid-curve), or ECDSA-level attacks that need leaked nonce bits (HNP/lattice/Fourier) — none of which attacks the ECDLP itself.

### Cited Findings

**secp256k1 parameters relevant to the attack conditions**
- secp256k1 has embedding degree 19298681539552699237261830834781317975472927379845817397100860523586360249056 (≈1.9×10^76), is not anomalous (n ≠ p), and its trace of Frobenius is 432420386565659656852420866390673177327 (snippet) — [Standard curve database, std.neuromancer.sk](https://std.neuromancer.sk/secg/secp256k1/); corroborated by [Elementary Bitcoin ch. 5](https://elementarybitcoin.org/chapters/05-secp256k1.html) and [S. Arango Vergara, "Trying to attack SECP256K1 (2025)", LinkedIn](https://translate.google.com/translate?u=https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/trying-attack-secp256k1-2025-sebastian-arango-vergara-s3fyc&hl=es&sl=en&tl=es&client=srp).
- secp256k1 has cofactor 1 (prime group order), but its twist has cofactor 3²·13²·3319·22639 (snippet, SafeCurves-derived) — [SafeCurves twist page](https://safecurves.cr.yp.to/twist.html) via search; the GitHub write-up gives the twist order of E': y²=x³+2 as 3·13²·3319·22639·1013176677300131846900870239606035638738100997248092069256697437031 (fully fetched) — [mathengem/twist, secp256k1 twist attacks (2020-05-26)](https://github.com/mathengem/twist/blob/master/2020_05_26_secp256k1_twist_attacks/secp256k1_twist_attacks.md). **Note the 3 vs 3² discrepancy between the two sources; the full-fetch GitHub factorization should be preferred but was not independently verified here.**
- SafeCurves criteria require embedding degree ≥ roughly 100 for a 256-bit n; secp256k1 meets this, while a weak curve might have k=6 enabling sub-exponential attacks (snippet) — [Computation of Trusted Short Weierstrass Elliptic Curves, arXiv 2208.01635](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.01635).
- Pollard rho breaks ECDLP with about 0.886·√ℓ additions on average (snippet) — [SafeCurves rho page](https://safecurves.cr.yp.to/rho.html) via [arXiv 2208.01635](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.01635).

**Generic lower bound (Shoup 1997)**
- Shoup's EUROCRYPT '97 paper considers "generic algorithms" that do not exploit any special properties of the encoding of group elements, and proves any generic algorithm must perform Ω(√p) group operations, where p is the largest prime dividing the group order; the bound is matched by Shanks' baby-step giant-step (snippet) — [Shoup, "Lower Bounds for Discrete Logarithms and Related Problems", Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-69053-0_18); [ACM DL entry](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/1754542.1754568).
- The generic-group model is still being refined; a 2026 paper introduces a "Structured Generic-Group Model" — [ePrint 2026/384](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/384). (Title only; content not fetched.)

**MOV / Frey–Rück pairing transfer**
- Pairing-based (MOV/Frey–Rück) attacks transfer the ECDLP into F_{p^k}^* and are only feasible for small embedding degree k; secp256k1's huge embedding degree and prime order mean these attacks do not apply (snippet) — [Elementary Bitcoin ch. 5](https://elementarybitcoin.org/chapters/05-secp256k1.html); [Standard curve database](https://std.neuromancer.sk/secg/secp256k1/).
- secp256k1 was "consciously chosen to avoid the known pitfalls" targeted by MOV, Frey–Rück, anomalous-curve attacks and Xedni calculus (snippet) — [Arango Vergara 2025](https://translate.google.com/translate?u=https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/trying-attack-secp256k1-2025-sebastian-arango-vergara-s3fyc&hl=es&sl=en&tl=es&client=srp).

**Anomalous-curve (Smart / Satoh–Araki / Semaev) attack**
- The attack requires #E(F_p) = p (trace 1); secp256k1 is listed "Anomalous: false" and n ≠ p, so the attack does not apply (snippet) — [Standard curve database](https://std.neuromancer.sk/secg/secp256k1/); [Elementary Bitcoin ch. 5](https://elementarybitcoin.org/chapters/05-secp256k1.html).

**Weil descent / GHS and summation-polynomial index calculus (extension-field methods)**
- The 2015 Galbraith–Gaudry survey reviews index-calculus algorithms using summation polynomials and the descent/GHS family — [Galbraith & Gaudry, "Recent progress on the ECDLP", ePrint 2015/1022](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/1022.pdf) (title/abstract snippet only).
- A mid-2026 state-of-the-art survey (community document, not peer reviewed) concludes: "All index calculus and descent attacks require extension fields for practical prime-field curves"; GHS, Joux–Vitse, Tian–Fan, Howe gluing and Richelot-isogeny descents are listed under extension-field methods; the "first-fall-degree assumption" used in binary-field summation-polynomial complexity claims is "now widely doubted"; prime-field index calculus (Pardini–Kummer–Mestre 2016, Amadori–Pintore–Sala variants) is "open-but-unmoved" (fully fetched) — [aburan28/crypto PR #19, "ECDLP state-of-the-art survey (2025–2026)"](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto/pull/19).
- The same survey notes a practical gap: Semaev-style index calculus processes "<1,000 systems/second vs. 25,000–150,000 point additions/second" for rho, so theoretical gains are offset by Gröbner-basis computational walls (fully fetched) — [aburan28/crypto PR #19](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto/pull/19).
- Semaev introduced summation polynomials in 2004 and proposed a prime-field factor base of points with small x-coordinates; approaches via rational maps and summation polynomials "appeared to be the first working case for curves defined over prime fields, but were only feasible for small parameters" (snippet) — [Implementation analysis of index calculus method on elliptic curves over prime finite fields, ePrint 2024/1923](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1923); [EasyChair preprint 15613](https://easychair.org/publications/preprint/6SDg/open).
- Semaev's 2015 paper claimed 2^{c√(n ln n)} (c≈1.69) for curves over F_{2^n} under a first-fall-degree assumption, and claimed better-than-Pollard performance for several FIPS binary curves (snippet) — [Semaev, ePrint 2015/310](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/310); [arXiv 1504.01175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01175). This is a **binary-field** claim.
- Galbraith & Gebregiyorgis generalized summation-polynomial attacks from binary to prime *extension* fields; their experiments indicated "Pollard rho is still much faster than index calculus algorithms for the ECDLP over prime extension fields of reasonable size" (snippet) — [ePrint 2014/806](https://eprint.iacr.org/2014/806).
- Petit, Kosters & Messeng (PKC 2016) gave a prime-field index-calculus approach that works only "when p − 1 has a smooth factor" (snippet) — [Algebraic Approaches for the ECDLP over Prime Fields, Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-49387-8_1); [author PDF](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf).
- Complexity bounds paper: "the naive index calculus method cannot be more efficient than the rho method over an arbitrary finite field, and based on experimental observations, it cannot be more efficient than even the brute force method" (snippet) — [Complexity bounds on Semaev's naive index calculus method for ECDLP, J. Math. Cryptology](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jmc-2019-0029/html).
- The largest Semaev summation polynomial ever computed is the 6th; the polynomials grow over-exponentially with extension degree (snippet) — [ePrint 2017/609, "On the discrete logarithm problem for prime-field elliptic curves"](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/609.pdf).
- 2018 Amadori–Pintore–Sala variant reduces the number of Gröbner-basis computations (snippet) — [Finite Fields and Their Applications 51 (2018)](https://unpaywall.org/10.1016/J.FFA.2018.01.009).

**GLV endomorphism and negation map (constant-factor only)**
- On a j=0 curve like secp256k1 the endomorphism (x,y)→(βx,y) is multiplication by a cube root of unity λ mod n; the rho walk can be folded by all six automorphisms for a √6 speed-up (negation map alone gives √2); "the gains from such automorphisms are modest – on the order of a constant factor like √d if there is an automorphism of order d" (snippet) — [aburan28/crypto PR #483](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto/pull/483); background on the endomorphism at [Paul Miller, noble-secp256k1](https://paulmillr.com/posts/noble-secp256k1-fast-ecc/).
- Correct use of the negation map in rho (avoiding fruitless cycles) is a known engineering issue — [Bos, Kleinjung, Lenstra, "On the Correct Use of the Negation Map in the Pollard rho Method", Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-19379-8_8) (title only).

**Twist and invalid-curve attacks (implementation-level)**
- The secp256k1 twist attack requires "ECDH-style key agreement" where the victim multiplies an attacker-supplied point *without validating it*; the attacker recovers the key modulo small twist-order factors (3319, 22639, 109903, 12977017, 383229727, ... across twists E1–E6) and combines via CRT; "the attack does not compromise ECDSA signature verification"; mitigation is on-curve checks or compressed keys (fully fetched) — [mathengem/twist write-up](https://github.com/mathengem/twist/blob/master/2020_05_26_secp256k1_twist_attacks/secp256k1_twist_attacks.md).
- "In Bitcoin's secp256k1 library, point validation is performed to mitigate this attack"; secp256k1's main group has no small subgroups because its order is prime (snippet) — [LayerX research, cryptographic well-known attacks](https://scrapbox.io/layerx/Cryptographic_well-known_attacks); [SafeCurves twist page](https://safecurves.cr.yp.to/twist.html).
- Tutorial-style "twist attack" repositories exist that recover keys "within 5–15 minutes" *given* a vulnerable implementation — [demining/Twist-Attack](https://github.com/demining/Twist-Attack). (These demonstrate the implementation bug, not an ECDLP weakness.)

**Lattice / hidden-number-problem attacks (ECDSA-level, need nonce information)**
- Breitner & Heninger (FC 2019) computed "hundreds of Bitcoin private keys and dozens of Ethereum, Ripple, SSH, and HTTPS private keys" from biased ECDSA nonces using a lattice HNP solver; the vulnerable Bitcoin keys held roughly $54 total (snippet) — [ePrint 2019/023](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/023); [ACRAB summary](https://acrab.isi.jhu.edu/s/1nz0y9/biased_nonce_sense_lattice_attacks).
- LadderLeak (CCS 2020, Aranha et al.) breaks ECDSA with less than one bit of nonce leakage via Fourier-analysis HNP, demonstrated on sect163r1 and NIST P-192 against a Montgomery-ladder timing flaw in OpenSSL (snippet) — [ePrint 2020/615](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/615.pdf); [ACM CCS 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3372297.3417268).
- Lattice-sieving HNP attacks continue to improve (ASIACRYPT 2024) — [Attacking ECDSA with Nonce Leakage by Lattice Sieving, Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-0944-4_1) (title only); a 2026 power side-channel variant — [Sleep Reveals the Nonce, arXiv 2602.01491](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.01491) (title only).

**Quantum (Shor)** — see Key Question 5.

### Inferences
- Because secp256k1 has embedding degree ≈1.9×10^76, trace ≠ 1, a prime (not extension) field, prime order, and a twist whose small factors only matter for unvalidated-input ECDH, none of the structural attacks in the literature changes its classical ECDLP cost from the generic ≈0.886·√n/√6 ≈ 2^126–2^127 group operations (the √6 is the only "structural" gain, and it is a constant factor).
- The GLV endomorphism (small CM discriminant −3) is the one SafeCurves-style criterion secp256k1 is commonly flagged on, but its effect is exactly the √6 rho fold — it does not change the exponent.
- Twist and HNP attacks are the only attack classes with real-world Bitcoin key losses on record, and both are implementation/protocol failures (missing point validation; biased nonces), not ECDLP results.

- I could not fetch SafeCurves directly, so secp256k1's exact SafeCurves "rho cost" (2^127.8 from memory — **unverified here**), its listed CM discriminant, and the twist-attack cost table are not cited with numbers.
- Shoup's own paper text was not fetched; the description of what the generic model excludes (index calculus in F_p^*, pairing transfers) is standard but only snippet-level here.
- No primary text of the Galbraith–Gaudry survey or the PKC 2016 Petit et al. paper could be fetched; the p−1 smoothness condition and conclusions are from snippets. Whether p−1 for secp256k1's p has a usable smooth factor was not checked in this session.

## Key Question 2: Best published record for solving ECDLP on a prime-field curve, and distance from 256 bits

### Takeaway
The best *full* prime-field ECDLP record found is 114 bits (Barreto–Naehrig curve with automorphisms, Pollard rho, ~2,000 CPU cores for ~6 months, 2017–18); the prior generic prime-field record is 112 bits (2009, >200 PlayStation 3s, 62.6 PS3-years). The largest binary-field record is 117.35 bits (sect113r2, FPGAs, 2016). Interval DLPs (Bitcoin puzzle #130, ~2^65 kangaroo operations, Sept 2024) are larger in nominal bits but are not full ECDLP. All are roughly 2^60–2^70 (10^18–10^21) times cheaper than a 256-bit instance.

### Cited Findings
- 112-bit prime-field record: Bos, Kaihara, Kleinjung, Lenstra, Montgomery; Pollard rho on a Cell-processor implementation; "required 62.6 PS3 years and ran on a PS3 cluster of more than 200 PS3s in the period January – July, 2009"; improvements from a branch-free negation-map rho and "sloppy" modular reduction (snippet) — [Solving a 112-bit prime ECDLP on game consoles using sloppy reduction, EPFL Infoscience](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/entities/publication/f7083e68-80a7-48f1-8653-1e5159bbb672); [Bos et al., Pollard Rho on the PlayStation 3 (PDF)](https://www.joppebos.com/files/rho_ps3.pdf); [Microsoft Research video](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/video/how-to-solve-a-112-bit-ecdlp-using-game-consoles/).
- 114-bit prime-field record: a Barreto–Naehrig curve solved with parallel Pollard rho, "2000 CPU cores taking about 6 months" (snippet) — [Solving 114-Bit ECDLP for a Barreto-Naehrig Curve, Springer (ACISP 2018)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-78556-1_13); announced on ellipticnews 22 April 2018 — [ellipticnews](https://ellipticnews.wordpress.com/2018/04/22/114-bit-ecdlp-solved-on-a-curve-with-automorphisms-over-a-prime-field/). (BN curves have j=0 and thus the same order-6 automorphism group as secp256k1, so the √6 fold was available.)
- 117.35-bit binary-field record (2016): Bernstein, Engels, Lange, Niederhagen, Paar, Schwabe, Zimmermann attacked the SECG curve sect113r2 on FPGAs, "a new size record for completed ECDL computations" with 3 rho cores per low-cost Spartan-6 FPGA (snippet) — [Faster elliptic-curve discrete logarithms on FPGAs, ePrint 2016/382](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/382).
- The 2025 "Brace for impact" paper (arXiv 2508.14011) states classical prime-field ECDLP records "cluster near 112–113 bits" and notes a mid-2010s binary-field record in the ~117-bit range (snippet) — [Brace for impact: ECDLP challenges for quantum cryptanalysis, arXiv 2508.14011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.14011). **Conflict:** this snippet's "112–113 bits" is inconsistent with the 114-bit BN record above; the 114-bit result may have been excluded because it is a special (pairing-friendly, automorphism-rich) curve.
- ECC2K-130 (Certicom Koblitz binary challenge, ~131-bit): a 2009 paper described a distributed rho attack with "2^60.9 iterations per year using 3039 3GHz Core 2 CPUs or 2716 GTX 295 GPUs or 2466 Cell CPUs or 2026 XC3S5000 FPGAs" (snippet) — [Breaking ECC2K-130, ePrint 2009/541](https://eprint.iacr.org/2009/541.pdf); Certicom originally called it "infeasible" (2.7×10^9 machine days) — [The Certicom Challenges ECC2-X](https://binary.cr.yp.to/ecc2x-20090901.pdf). **Conflict:** one search snippet said the challenge was "definitively solved in 2010", another said it is "the smallest unsolved Certicom discrete-logarithm challenge". I found no primary announcement of a solution; treat "unsolved" as the safer reading pending a check of [ecc-challenge.info](https://ecc-challenge.info/) (blocked here).
- Interval DLP (not full ECDLP): Bitcoin puzzle #130's public key was exposed, allowing Pollard kangaroo at "about 2^65 operations instead of 2^130"; it was "solved in September 2024" (snippet) — [btcpuzzlesearch.com/solved](https://btcpuzzlesearch.com/solved); [Kangaroo pool thread, bitcointalk](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=5500311.0).
- Puzzle #135 (public key known, ~2^67 kangaroo operations) "remains unsolved" as of mid-2026 (snippet) — [Mizogg puzzle guide](https://mizogg.com/learn/puzzle-solving.html); [news.bitcoin.com, 916 BTC unsolved](https://news.bitcoin.com/key-hunters-eye-58-87m-bitcoin-puzzle-as-916-btc-sits-unsolved-in-78-addresses/).
- Community survey summary: "Full ECDLP records: ~112–117 bits; Bitcoin puzzle interval solves: ~130-bit intervals using ~2^65 operations; security gap vs. 256-bit curves: approximately 10^19× advantage needed" (fully fetched) — [aburan28/crypto PR #19](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto/pull/19).

### Inferences
- A generic 256-bit ECDLP costs ≈0.886·2^128/√6 ≈ 2^126.5 group operations. The 114-bit record cost on the order of 2^56–2^57 operations; the ratio is ≈2^70 ≈ 10^21. Against the 2^65 puzzle-#130 kangaroo solve the ratio is ≈2^61–2^63 ≈ 10^18–10^19, matching the community survey's "10^19×".
- Records have advanced only ~2 bits in the prime-field case since 2009 (112→114) and none since 2018 despite a decade of GPU growth; the frontier is moving at a pace consistent with Moore's-law-style constant-factor gains, not algorithmic change.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the "Brace for impact" table directly; exact years/teams for the ~113-bit entry it references were not confirmed.
- No 2020–2026 full-ECDLP record on any prime-field curve was found in search; absence of evidence, not confirmed absence (Wikipedia's records page was blocked).
- Exact operation counts and hardware for puzzle #130 come from puzzle-community sites, not a peer-reviewed source.

## Key Question 3: 2024–2026 papers or preprints claiming new sub-exponential progress on prime-field ECDLP, and their reception

### Takeaway
I found no 2024–2026 paper that credibly claims, or that the community accepts as, a sub-exponential classical algorithm for prime-field ECDLP. The nearest items are (a) the Abdullah–Mahalanobis "zero minor / Las Vegas" line (2020 → Experimental Mathematics 2025 → "Guess and Determine" arXiv July 2026), which reduces ECDLP to a linear-algebra search whose hard step is conjectural and which has only been demonstrated up to group order ~2^50, and (b) implementation studies of prime-field index calculus (ePrint 2024/1923) that confirm it is feasible only for small parameters. Community surveys in 2026 continue to state that no sub-exponential classical algorithm exists for prime-field ECDLP.

### Cited Findings
- Abdullah, Mahalanobis (& Mallick): a Las Vegas algorithm reduces ECDLP to finding a zero minor in a non-singular matrix; the "initial minors" conjecture posits a sub-exponential set of minors whose non-vanishing implies all minors are non-zero; experiments verified it "for groups of order up to 2^50"; the authors "claim there is a successful attack in the making" (snippet) — [Minors solve the ECDLP, arXiv 2310.04132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04132); [A new method for solving the ECDLP, Groups Complexity Cryptology](https://gcc.episciences.org/7180); published as "A Zero Minor Solves the ECDLP" — [Experimental Mathematics (2025)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10586458.2025.2525844).
- Mahalanobis, "A Guess and Determine Attack on the ECDLP" (arXiv 2607.09814, submitted 10 July 2026, revised 6 Aug 2026, IISER Pune): guesses part of a maximal zero-minor and determines the rest via the intersection poset of a hyperplane arrangement; "for a chosen elliptic curve, the worst case complexity of the determine step of the attack is in polynomial time" (snippet) — [arXiv 2607.09814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09814). **No overall complexity, no record instance, and no claim about secp256k1 appear in the snippet; the guess step's cost is not stated.**
- ePrint 2024/1923 (implementation analysis of index calculus over prime fields): summation-polynomial approaches "were only feasible for small parameters"; the 2018 Amadori et al. variant reduces Gröbner computations (snippet) — [ePrint 2024/1923](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1923).
- Community survey (mid-2026): "Best-known generic attack remains O(√n)... No subexponential classical algorithm exists for prime-field ECDLP"; prime-field index calculus status "open-but-unmoved"; first-fall-degree assumption "widely doubted" (fully fetched) — [aburan28/crypto PR #19](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto/pull/19).
- A 2026 automated review of 131 ECDLP-over-prime-field proposals found "no sub-exponential ECDLP breakthroughs"; one headline result was "mathematically void", one was refuted by a counterexample at p=13, and 39 pre-registered discriminators "could not fire" for algebraic reasons (fully fetched) — [aburan28/crypto-autoresearcher PR #719](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto-autoresearcher/pull/719).
- A search for a refuted 2025 "polynomial-time ECDLP" ePrint returned only an unrelated HAWK-lattice retraction ("HAWK 'Guessing Game' is not Polynomial-Time") — [ePrint 2026/1377](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1377). No ECDLP analogue surfaced.
- The 2024 Chinese D-Wave "RSA broken" episode (22-bit RSA on a quantum annealer, later 50- and 90-bit hybrid results) was widely judged overblown by experts and did not involve ECC (snippet) — [Forbes, "No, China hasn't broken military encryption"](https://www.forbes.com/sites/craigsmith/2024/10/16/department-of-anti-hype-no-china-hasnt-broken-military-encryption-with-quantum-computers/); [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchsecurity/news/366613737/Experts-slam-Chinese-research-on-quantum-encryption-attack).

### Inferences
- The Mahalanobis line is the only sustained, peer-reviewed-venue attempt at a new prime-field ECDLP algorithm in the window; its published evidence (≤2^50) is ~2^60 below existing rho records, and the polynomial claim applies to one sub-step only. It should be classed as "speculative research direction", not "progress".
- The absence of any ellipticnews/ePrint controversy in search results for 2024–2026 (unlike 2015, when Semaev's claim drew immediate rebuttals) suggests no widely-noticed sub-exponential claim was made.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the arXiv/Experimental Mathematics text to check whether the authors compare against Pollard rho or state a total complexity; no independent critique of the zero-minor method was found in search.
- ePrint listings for 2024–2026 could not be browsed directly (blocked), so a low-profile crank or flawed preprint could have been missed.

## Key Question 4: "AI/LLM discovers ECDLP shortcut" claims (2025–2026) and their status

### Takeaway
No AI system has produced a classical ECDLP shortcut. The three relevant items are: (1) "Mage" (Dec 2025), a 784M-parameter transformer that memorizes training key-pairs and overfits, whose extrapolated threat claims are unsupported by its own held-out results; (2) Epoch AI's FrontierMath open ECDLP problem, designed so that a solve would signal a new attack — no solve reported; and (3) LLM-agent "autoresearch" projects (Eigen Labs' ECDSA.Fail, aburan28's crypto-autoresearcher) which optimized *quantum* Shor circuits or systematically falsified classical proposals — not a break.

### Cited Findings
- Mage (arXiv 2512.12483): a 784M-parameter Cross-Axis Transformer (hidden 2048, 16 layers, 16 heads) trained on 100,000 key pairs and evaluated on 38,200; the model achieved "99% accuracy on the training set, however, as training loss decreased, evaluation loss increased, indicating overfitting"; the authors nevertheless claim a 784M model "could memorise enough keypairs to compromise half of all private keys with an effort equivalent to breaking only 100 keys" and speculate about a 405B-parameter model (snippet) — [arXiv 2512.12483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12483); [Quantum Zeitgeist coverage](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/security-mage-enables-cracking-elliptic-curve-cryptography-risks-modern/). **The curve size tested and any held-out (non-training) success rate were not in the snippet; the overfitting statement is the authors' own.**
- Epoch AI FrontierMath open problem: recover x from (p,a,b,P,Q); "the instance ... is chosen to be hard, but not quite as hard as instances used in real cryptographic protocols"; "if AI can solve this problem in an hour, it would suggest that the AI found a new attack or exploited some structure"; verifier checks Q = nP (snippet) — [Epoch AI, ECDLP open problem](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems/elliptic-curve-discrete-logarithm). No report of a solve appeared in search.
- ECDSA.Fail (Eigen Labs, launched late May 2026): "LLM-based agents, including general-purpose coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex" and >100 participants optimized a reversible secp256k1 mixed point-addition circuit for Shor's algorithm, cutting the score by 86.1% (snippet) — [ECDSA.Fail: Open Autoresearch..., arXiv 2609.09582](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09582); contest repo — [ecdlp-contest/ecdlp-point-add](https://github.com/ecdlp-contest/ecdlp-point-add). This is quantum-circuit optimization, not a classical attack.
- crypto-autoresearcher (aburan28): coordinated Claude agents design and blind-review experiments on 131 prime-field ECDLP proposals; outcome "No sub-exponential ECDLP breakthroughs emerged"; the process instead surfaced a √2 ambiguity in the rho baseline constant (whether 0.886 includes the negation map) (fully fetched) — [PR #719](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto-autoresearcher/pull/719).
- Google's March 2026 whitepaper withheld its circuits behind a zero-knowledge proof; André Schrottenloher independently reproduced them within two months, and by end of June 2026 the open ECDSA.Fail network had a circuit "47.5 percent more efficient than Google's" (snippet) — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/google-quantum-cryptography-zero-knowledge); [postquantum.com](https://postquantum.com/security-pqc/google-ecdlp-circuits-reproduced-open/).
- Searches for "AI broke secp256k1 debunked" returned nothing beyond the quantum-circuit work — [search context](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09582v1).

### Inferences
- Mage's methodology (train/eval on random keypairs) cannot generalize: private keys are uniformly random and independent of the public-key encoding under any learned map cheaper than ECDLP; its 99%-train/worsening-eval profile is textbook memorization. The extrapolation to "billions of keys" is speculation by the authors and should be treated as such.
- The concrete impact of LLM agents in 2026 has been on the *quantum* side (shrinking Shor circuit cost by tens of percent), which shortens the quantum timeline modestly but leaves the classical picture untouched.

### Gaps
- Mage's authors/affiliation, tested curve size, and any peer review or rebuttal were not retrievable (arXiv, ResearchGate, Quantum Zeitgeist all blocked).
- Whether any frontier model has attempted the FrontierMath ECDLP instance, and with what result, is not reported in accessible sources.

## Key Question 5: Realistic quantum timeline for breaking secp256k1 (logical/physical qubit estimates from recent papers)

### Takeaway
Between March and September 2026 the resource estimates for Shor on secp256k1 dropped sharply: Google Quantum AI (31 Mar 2026) gives ≤1,200 logical qubits / ≤90M Toffoli (or ≤1,450 / ≤70M) and <500,000 physical superconducting qubits at 10^-3 error, with a ~9-minute online phase; EUROCRYPT 2026 (Chevignard–Fouque–Schrottenloher) reaches ~1,098–1,193 logical qubits with a large gate penalty; Luo et al. (July 2026) reach 835 logical qubits; IonQ (Sept 2026) claims a fully compiled trapped-ion qLDPC blueprint at 19,397 physical / 1,457 logical qubits, 39M Toffoli, 25.7 days per attempt. The largest ECDLP actually run on quantum hardware is 15 bits (April 2026). 2026 machines have ~1,000–1,200 physical qubits and tens-to-~100 logical qubits; a June 2026 forecasting paper puts a cryptographically relevant quantum computer at ~1/6 probability by 2035, ~30% by 2040, ~60% by 2050.

### Cited Findings
- Google Quantum AI whitepaper "Securing Elliptic Curve Cryptocurrencies against Quantum Vulnerabilities: Resource Estimates and Mitigations" (57 pages, 31 March 2026; co-authors from Ethereum Foundation incl. Justin Drake and Stanford's Dan Boneh; arXiv 2603.28846): low-qubit variant ≤1,200 logical qubits and ≤90M Toffoli; low-gate variant ≤1,450 logical and ≤70M Toffoli; "fewer than 500,000 physical qubits" on a superconducting surface-code architecture with planar degree-4 connectivity, 10^-3 physical error, 1 µs code cycle, 10 µs reaction time; ~10× spacetime improvement over prior single-instance estimates (snippet) — [Google whitepaper PDF](https://quantumai.google/static/site-assets/downloads/cryptocurrency-whitepaper.pdf); [arXiv 2603.28846](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.28846); [Conor Deegan summary on X](https://x.com/conordeegan/status/2038852537290260746); [postquantum.com](https://postquantum.com/security-pqc/google-quantum-bitcoin-ecdlp/).
- The "9 minutes" figure: fixed-curve precomputation can be done in advance so that "the remaining computation [takes] about 9 minutes once a target public key appears" (snippet) — [Safeheron blog](https://safeheron.com/blog/google-quantum-whitepaper/); [Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/49018/bitcoin-private-key-quantum-computer-9-minutes-google-quantum-ai-paper); [CoinDesk, 31 Mar 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/03/31/bitcoin-bulls-scramble-for-post-quantum-protection-as-google-drops-bombshell-paper).

- Timeline forecast: Gershteyn & Alber, "Quantum Horizon" (4 June 2026): "about a one-in-six chance by 2035, near 30% by 2040, and about 60% by 2050" for a cryptographically relevant quantum computer; of Bitcoin's ~6M quantum-exposed coins "only about 2.3 million are irreducibly at risk" (snippet) — [arXiv 2606.14484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14484).

### Inferences
- The logical-qubit floor for ECDLP-256 fell from ~2,330 (2017) → ~2,124 (2020) → ~1,100–1,200 (early 2026) → 835 (July 2026), i.e. ~3× in nine years with most of the drop in 2026; Toffoli counts (tens of millions) and physical-qubit needs (0.02M–0.5M depending on code/hardware assumptions) are now the binding constraints, not logical width.
- The gap between demonstrated capability (15-bit ECDLP; ~10^2 logical qubits) and requirement (256-bit; ~10^3 logical qubits with ~10^-11 logical error over 10^7–10^8 Toffolis) is at least one order of magnitude in qubit count and many orders in error-corrected gate volume; no source found claims a machine meeting Google's or IonQ's assumptions exists or is scheduled before ~2030.
- Because the online phase after public-key exposure could be minutes (Google) to weeks (IonQ), the practical exposure model for secp256k1 is "keys whose public key is on-chain" — the same population the puzzle/kangaroo community already targets classically.

### Gaps
- None of the 2026 quantum papers could be read in full; the assumed physical error rates, decoder assumptions, and precise Toffoli counts are snippet-level and in places inconsistent across secondary sources (1,098 vs 1,193 for Chevignard et al.; 70M vs ~2M Toffoli quoted for Google's reproduced circuits in one snippet, which likely refers to a different sub-metric).
- Roetteler et al. 2017 and Litinski 2023 figures were not directly confirmed in this session.
- No source gave a physical-qubit or runtime estimate for the 835-logical-qubit construction.
